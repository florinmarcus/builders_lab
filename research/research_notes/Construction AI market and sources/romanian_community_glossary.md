# Romanian community in UK construction, and a Romanian-English builder glossary (as of Nov 2026)

Notes compiled 7 Nov 2026. No personal data about individuals is included. Companies House examples are cited only as proof that the pattern exists, not as profiles.

## (a1) How many Romanians work in UK construction (employees, self-employed, company owners)?

### Takeaway
Romanians are the largest non-UK nationality in UK construction. Recent estimates are about 5% of the national construction workforce and about 19% in London (CITB analysis cited in a June 2026 ACORD UK / Bournemouth University report), and 6% of the on-site housebuilding workforce (HBF census 2023). No official headcount split into employees, sole traders and company owners exists in what I found. The last useful self-employment figure is ONS 2014–2016 for "EU2" (Romania plus Bulgaria): 66% self-employed.

### Cited Findings
**Headcounts and shares**
- CITB/Warwick research: Romanians were the largest national group in UK construction in 2017, about 63,600 workers. That was up from about 27,000 in 2015. Accessed via search snippet of the CITB migration research executive summary (the PDF now returns 404) — [CITB exec summary (dead link, Nov 2026)](https://www.citb.co.uk/media/wori34p1/citb_migration-research_exec-summary.pdf); project page: [Warwick IER, Migration and construction 2018](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/ier/researchthemesoverview/researchprojects/migrationconstruction2018/)
- 2026 policy report *The Romanian Diaspora's Contributions to the British Economy and Society* (ACORD UK and Bournemouth University, about June 2026). It cites CITB analysis that Romania is the largest foreign source of UK construction workers, at about 5% nationally and about 19% in London — [Bournemouth staff profile entry for the report](https://staffprofiles.bournemouth.ac.uk/display/report/518895); [eprints PDF record](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/42193/); summary via search of [Romania Insider, 2026](https://www.romania-insider.com/romanian-diaspora-uk-valuable-workforces-report-2026) (page returned HTTP 429 when fetched)
- The same report, as covered by undelucram.ro (18 Aug 2026), says Romanians are 5% of the British construction workforce, and the next foreign nationality is under 1%. It also says more than 2,000 Romanian workers are on site at Hinkley Point C — [undelucram.ro, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.undelucram.ro/en/cumlucram/1-million-romanians-in-the-uk-what-the-workforce-numbers-mean-for-employees-and-recruiters-2435)
- Profit.ro (14 Jul 2026), reporting the same study, says Romanians are 19% of construction workers in London and Romania is the largest source of foreign construction labour — [Profit.ro, 14 Jul 2026](https://profit.ro/perspective/romanii-au-devenit-motorul-invizibil-in-marea-britanie-parasesc-insa-accelerat-regatul-ritm-de-plecare-mai-mare-decat-al-oricarei-alte-nationalitati-din-ue-22516305)
- HBF Workforce Census 2023 covers on-site housebuilding only: over 14,000 participants. 19.6% of respondents were from overseas (15.8% EU/EEA). Romanians were the largest non-UK group at 6% of the whole workforce, or 37% of the non-UK workforce. Next were Indian (0.87%), Polish (0.79%), Lithuanian (0.78%) and Albanian (0.70%). In London, 50.8% of the housebuilding workforce were EU/EEA nationals and 13.7% were other overseas nationals. The trades with the most non-UK workers were general labourers (28%), plasterers (25%) and painters and decorators (25%), against 8% in plumbing — [HBF Workforce Census 2023 (PDF, June 2023)](https://www.hbf.co.uk/documents/12623/HBF_Workforce_Census_2023.pdf)

**Self-employment**
- ONS (published Aug 2018, from APS 2014–2016): 41% of all construction workers were self-employed, against 56% of non-UK workers and 39% of UK-born workers. Self-employment was highest among EU8 (63%) and EU2 (Bulgaria/Romania, 66%) workers. 29% of migrant construction workers were EU2, against 9% of migrants in other industries. In London, 28% of construction workers were EU27-born — [ESTEEM summary of the ONS report, Aug 2018](https://esteem.sites.sheffield.ac.uk/english/esteem-resources/knowledge-base/report-from-the-ons)
- The 2026 Bournemouth/ACORD coverage describes a large Romanian self-employed and micro-business sector "mainly in construction, logistics and tech". It gives no figure, and it recommends better self-employment data — [Profit.ro, 14 Jul 2026](https://profit.ro/perspective/romanii-au-devenit-motorul-invizibil-in-marea-britanie-parasesc-insa-accelerat-regatul-ritm-de-plecare-mai-mare-decat-al-oricarei-alte-nationalitati-din-ue-22516305)
- HMRC payroll data recorded 349,700 payrolled employments held by Romanian nationals in December 2025. This is a search-snippet attribution to a Bournemouth-linked page, and it covers all sectors, not construction only — [Bournemouth University page](https://www.bournemouth.ac.uk/node/113391)

**Company owners**
- Companies House shows many small construction Ltds whose directors or PSCs list Romanian nationality, for example companies named "... Construct Ltd" or "... Construction Ltd" incorporated 2015–2022. Search results showed several such officer and PSC pages. I found no aggregate count — e.g. [Companies House search example (company 14069541 officers)](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/14069541/officers)

**Wider population context**
- EUSS: by June 2023 Romanians were the largest applicant nationality, with 1,538,980 applications out of 7.4 million — [gov.uk EUSS quarterly statistics, June 2023](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/eu-settlement-scheme-quarterly-statistics-june-2023/eu-settlement-scheme-quarterly-statistics-june-2023). By September 2023 the figure was 1,607,500, of which 522,080 were made after 30 June 2021 — [gov.uk EUSS quarterly statistics, Sep 2023](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/eu-settlement-scheme-quarterly-statistics-september-2023/eu-settlement-scheme-quarterly-statistics-september-2023)
- Profit.ro (Jul 2026) gives 435,720 Romanians with settled status and 670,560 with pre-settled status. It says over 1.3 million Romanians hold some UK resident status, the fourth-largest foreign-born community. It also cites ONS figures: about 37,000 Romanians left and about 14,000 arrived in January–June 2025, the highest departure rate of any EU nationality — [Profit.ro, 14 Jul 2026](https://profit.ro/perspective/romanii-au-devenit-motorul-invizibil-in-marea-britanie-parasesc-insa-accelerat-regatul-ritm-de-plecare-mai-mare-decat-al-oricarei-alte-nationalitati-din-ue-22516305). Other coverage of the same report says "more than 1 million" — [undelucram.ro, Aug 2026](https://www.undelucram.ro/en/cumlucram/1-million-romanians-in-the-uk-what-the-workforce-numbers-mean-for-employees-and-recruiters-2435). (Conflict: 1.0m vs 1.3m+, depending on the measure.)
- EUSS applications are not people: Romanians had the most repeat applications at June 2023 (309,680, 29% of repeats) — [gov.uk EUSS June 2023](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/eu-settlement-scheme-quarterly-statistics-june-2023/eu-settlement-scheme-quarterly-statistics-june-2023)

### Inferences
- If the UK construction workforce is about 2.1–2.2 million (ONS 2014–16 put it at about 2.2m), 5% means roughly 100,000–110,000 Romanian construction workers. That is a back-of-envelope figure, not a published one.
- Applying the old EU2 self-employment rate (66%) suggests most Romanian builders are sole traders paid under CIS, but the rate is about a decade old. Post-2021 IR35/CIS enforcement and Ltd-company formation may have shifted the mix.
- Romanian presence is strongest in finishing and labour trades (labourer, plastering, decorating, drylining) and in London and the South East. It is weaker in licensed trades (gas, electrics). The HBF trade data supports this for housebuilding.
- Net outflow since 2024 means 2026 numbers may be lower than the 2023 HBF snapshot.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) ONS/APS table of Romanian nationals by industry found. Next step: an ONS ad-hoc request or LFS microdata.
- No count of Romanian-owned construction companies. Companies House bulk data (officer nationality + SIC 41/42/43) would allow one, but that needs a data pull, not a web search.
- The current CITB figure was seen only second-hand (via the Bournemouth report). The original CITB 2017 PDF link is dead.
- Regional split outside London (e.g. Midlands, Kent, Essex, Hinkley/Somerset) not found.

## (a2) Language: how many Romanian builders have limited English? Studies on safety and language barriers

### Takeaway
I found no study that measures English proficiency among Romanian construction workers specifically. UK evidence is general: HSE-commissioned and academic work flags language as a main safety barrier for migrant site workers, and HSE has prosecuted training delivered only in English.

### Cited Findings
- HSE-commissioned survey (2006) of 200 migrant workers in England and Wales, across sectors: English skills were "only occasionally demanded"; many had no training, though all reported a first-day site induction — [Personnel Today coverage](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/employers-failings-put-lives-of-migrant-contruction-staff-at-risk)
- HSE and TUC published multilingual material because many migrant workers had "only a limited grasp of English" and safety training was not being understood — [Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/?p=43839)
- A UK PhD thesis (Hull) on health, safety and environmental management of migrant construction workers: principal contractors named the language barrier as a main negative. It recommends information in workers' own language — [Greef, Hull repository](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/output/4221607/an-investigation-into-the-health-safety-and-environmental-management-of-migrant-workers-engaged-in-the-construction-industry-in-the-united-kingdon-uk)
- A law-firm summary says around half of London construction workers are non-UK born, and cites an HSE case where "training was provided only in English" was among the failings — [Enable Law](https://www.enablelaw.com/news-and-insights/protecting-migrant-workers-from-workplace-injury/)
- The 2026 Bournemouth report: Romanian is the second most spoken foreign language in London and Wales, a third of Romanian adults in England and Wales hold degree-level qualifications, and 39% of Romanian workers are in jobs below their qualification level — [undelucram.ro, Aug 2026](https://www.undelucram.ro/en/cumlucram/1-million-romanians-in-the-uk-what-the-workforce-numbers-mean-for-employees-and-recruiters-2435); [Profit.ro, Jul 2026](https://profit.ro/perspective/romanii-au-devenit-motorul-invizibil-in-marea-britanie-parasesc-insa-accelerat-regatul-ritm-de-plecare-mai-mare-decat-al-oricarei-alte-nationalitati-din-ue-22516305)
- GLA construction industry profile covers labour exploitation risks for migrant workers in construction — [GLAA industry profile: construction (PDF)](https://www.gla.gov.uk/media/5073/industry-profile-construction.pdf)

### Inferences
- English ability is mixed. Many Romanian builders run their own businesses and deal with UK clients, but plenty, especially newer arrivals and labourers, likely work through a Romanian-speaking gang leader or boss. A bilingual glossary and Romanian-language tools for quotes, invoices, CIS and RAMS probably meet a real need. This is an inference, not measured.

### Gaps
- No CITB or HSE figure on the share of Romanian (or EU2) site workers with limited English. ONS Census 2021 "main language" and "English proficiency" tables by Romanian main language could give a population-level proxy (not fetched).

## (a3) How Romanians find work and information, and where they gather online

### Takeaway
The channels are mostly informal: Facebook groups, WhatsApp, word of mouth and Romanian diaspora media. Formal anchors include a chamber of commerce and community associations. I could not verify member counts for named Facebook groups. Facebook group pages are not indexed by the search tools used.

### Cited Findings
- Romanian-language UK media include Gazeta Românească and Ziarul Românesc (listed in the Romanian Cultural Centre's UK guide) — [Romanian Cultural Centre guide](https://www.romanianculturalcentre.org.uk/guide)
- British Romanian Chamber of Commerce (BRCC), which runs a Facebook presence and news — [BRCC](https://brcconline.eu/)
- "My Romania Community", a Brent-based association founded in 2015 to support Romanians in the UK, including labour-market integration — [Brent Council community directory](https://www.brent.gov.uk/en/neighbourhoods-and-communities/community-directory/my-romania-community)
- ACORD UK (Advisory Council of the Romanian Diaspora in the UK) co-authored the 2026 diaspora report — [Bournemouth staff profile entry](https://staffprofiles.bournemouth.ac.uk/display/report/518895)
- Romanian tradespeople are visible on UK lead-generation sites such as MyJobQuote, offering plasterboard and skimming — [MyJobQuote tradesperson listing example](https://www.myjobquote.co.uk/t/roman-41)
- Romanian press shows Facebook construction groups used to hire crews, with problems (story set in Romania, not the UK) — [BZI.ro](https://www.bzi.ro/ce-a-patit-un-roman-care-a-angajat-o-echipa-de-muncitori-de-pe-facebook-sunt-vai-si-amar-o-nenorocire-fratilor-4591634)
- Romanian builders' merchant presence: VeroTherm, a Romanian polystyrene and dry-mortar maker, announced in Nov 2023 a London logistics and sales point near Wembley selling its own branded EWI and mortar products. I found no confirmation that it opened — [Romania Insider, Nov 2023](https://www.romania-insider.com/business/verotherm-logistics-center-london-2023)
- Romanian forum threads (avocatnet.ro) discuss UK site work and "self employed" status — [avocatnet.ro forum](https://www.avocatnet.ro/forum/discutie_14530/angajare-in-UK.html)

### Inferences
- Likely high-traffic places (unverified; check by hand on Facebook): general diaspora groups ("Români în Londra", "Români în UK", "Locuri de muncă UK pentru români"), trade groups ("Constructori români în UK/Anglia") and city groups (Birmingham, Manchester, Leicester, Luton, Harrow). WhatsApp groups per gang or site are the norm. Telegram is less used.
- Romanian supermarkets and shops (e.g. in Harrow, Burnt Oak, Ilford, Romford) and churches are offline notice-board channels.

### Gaps
- Member counts of named Facebook groups: not retrievable via web search. They need a manual check while logged in.
- No Romanian-run UK builders' merchant chain confirmed.
- No study found on Romanian builders' use of WhatsApp/Telegram.

## (b) Starter Romanian-English builder glossary

### Takeaway
Below are about 95 terms. A few rows are backed by sources (CIS/UTR rules, Rigips = plasterboard/drywall, skimming, builders' merchant). Most rows are compiled from general bilingual construction knowledge and need a native-speaker and UK-tradesperson check before use. Romglish/slang rows are marked "(colloquial, unverified)": I found no published source on UK Romanian site slang.

### Cited Findings
- CIS: contractors deduct 20% from registered subcontractors and 30% from unregistered ones. CIS registration is separate from self-employed (Self Assessment) registration, but both can be done together. The contractor asks for the UTR before the first payment. The UTR is a 10-digit number — [TaxAid](https://taxaid.org.uk/register/page/3); [LITRG on CIS](https://www.litrg.org.uk/working/self-employment/construction-industry-scheme-cis); [HMRC CIS340/CIS301](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/676438734e2d5e9c0bde9c00/CIS301.pdf)
- CIS registration alone does not prove self-employment. If the status is not genuine, the worker should be paid via PAYE — [TaxAid](https://taxaid.org.uk/register/page/3)
- "Rigips" is a brand. Translators give "drywall" (US) as the English for gips-carton. In the UK the term is plasterboard — [ProZ glossary](https://wiki.proz.com/kudoz/1902774)
- "Skimming" in the UK is the thin finish coat of plaster over plasterboard or existing plaster (≈ Romanian glet) — [MyJobQuote listing](https://www.myjobquote.co.uk/t/roman-41)
- "Builders' merchants" ≈ "furnizori de materiale de construcție" (ProZ) — [ProZ](https://vie.proz.com/kudoz/english-to-romanian/business-commerce-general/7154725-builders-merchants.html)
