# Paperwork and day-to-day admin of UK building businesses at three levels (2026)

Scope: England/UK. Three levels: (1) design-and-build firms (example: Robert's 21st Century Design, Hatfield), (2) small builders with a crew of 2-10, (3) sole tradespeople. Research done 7 Nov 2026. Claims marked **[UNVERIFIED]** came from my background knowledge or from search snippets I could not confirm against a primary page. Several gov.uk deep links returned 404 during this session (gov.uk restructures URLs), so some legal detail comes from secondary sources (accountants, law firms) and is flagged as such.

## Documents per level: what they produce and handle

### Takeaway
All three levels share a core of quotes, invoices, client records, insurance and photos. CIS and the CDM "domestic client" rule are what push a small builder into real compliance paperwork; a design-and-build firm adds design, planning, building control, principal designer/contractor duties, payment applications, variations and handover packs on top, plus accreditation (CHAS/CHAS Elite) audits. A sole trader's paperwork is lighter but includes competent-person certificates (Part P, Gas Safe) that are mandatory for their trade.

### Cited Findings

**Example firm (Level 1)**
- Robert's 21st Century Design: Hatfield office (Suite F, Andre House, Salisbury Square, AL9 5BT), "trusted by Hertfordshire homeowners and businesses since 2008"; luxury new builds, bespoke refurbishments, high-end renovations and commercial projects; says it handles architectural design, planning approval, construction and interior design, with "detailed planning" and "tailored project management"; shows CHAS and CHAS Elite logos and Best of Houzz awards 2016-2024; the home page does not mention building regulations or warranties. — [roberts21stcenturydesign.com](https://roberts21stcenturydesign.com/)
- Inference from the above: because it does design and build, it can sit in both the principal designer and principal contractor roles under CDM for domestic clients (see CDM below). Not stated on the site.

**CDM 2015 and the domestic client rule (Levels 1 and 2, sometimes 3)**
- For domestic clients, client duties pass to others: on a single-contractor project the contractor takes on the client duties (HSE says this "should involve little more than what they normally do in managing health and safety risks"); on a multi-contractor project the principal contractor takes them on, or the contractor in control if none is appointed. — [HSE: domestic clients](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm)
- A domestic client can, by written agreement, have a designer (e.g. architect) act as principal designer and take on client duties; without a written agreement, client duties "automatically pass to the principal contractor". — [HSE: domestic clients](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm)
- HSE links a "Construction Phase Plan (CDM 2015) - CIS80" guide from the domestic clients page. — [HSE: domestic clients](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm)
- **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]** CDM 2015 requires a construction phase plan for every construction project, however small (drawn up by the contractor on single-contractor jobs or the principal contractor on multi-contractor jobs). A health and safety file is required on projects with more than one contractor. I could not fetch the regulation text in this session.
- F10 notification to HSE is needed if work lasts more than 30 working days with more than 20 workers at once, or exceeds 500 person days; for domestic projects the contractor/principal contractor notifies. Sources disagree on "working days" vs "days" in HSE's own form help. — [Designing Buildings: F10](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/F10_notification); [HSE F10 form help](https://extranet.hse.gov.uk/forms/F10/Form_Help.htm)

**CIS (Levels 1 and 2 as contractors; Levels 2 and 3 as subcontractors)**
- Contractors must register if they pay subcontractors for construction work; subcontractors are not required to register but have deductions taken "at a higher rate" if not; firms can be both. Deductions count as advance payments towards the subcontractor's tax and NI. Work covered includes most building work; architecture/surveying, scaffold hire without labour, carpet fitting and material delivery are excluded. — [gov.uk: What is CIS](https://www.gov.uk/what-is-the-construction-industry-scheme)
- Contractors must verify subcontractors with HMRC, make deductions, file monthly returns and keep full records; penalties apply for missing returns or records. — [gov.uk: CIS contractor duties](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor)
- Rates: 20% registered, 30% unregistered, 0% for gross payment status; monthly return due by the 19th after the tax month (6th to 5th); nil return needed for months with no payments (unless HMRC told the business is inactive); payment due by the 22nd electronically (19th by post); £100 penalty for a return one day late, a further £200 after two months, charged per return. Secondary sources (accounting firms) — [HMRC print page via search snippet](https://www.hmrc.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor/print); [GoFile: CIS penalties](https://gofile.co.uk/knowledgebase/construction-industry-scheme/penalties/); [GoFile: CIS deadlines](https://gofile.co.uk/knowledgebase/construction-industry-scheme/filing-deadlines/)
- Payment and deduction statements to subcontractors: sources give the 19th of the month as the deadline. **[UNVERIFIED]** against HMRC directly. — [GoFile: CIS deadlines](https://gofile.co.uk/knowledgebase/construction-industry-scheme/filing-deadlines/)
- Note: CIS applies to contractor-subcontractor payments, not to a homeowner paying a builder (homeowners are not contractors under CIS). **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge; consistent with the gov.uk definition above]**

**VAT domestic reverse charge (DRC)**
- Applies to most building and construction services at standard and reduced rates when both parties are UK VAT-registered and the supply is reportable under CIS; covers construction, alteration, repair, demolition, installing building services, painting and decorating; excludes e.g. professional services of architects and surveyors supplied on their own. — [gov.uk: VAT DRC](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-domestic-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services)
- DRC does not apply to private homeowners (not VAT-registered); builder charges VAT normally. Where DRC applies, invoices show net amount and must include the words "reverse charge" (a £0 VAT line with no statement is non-compliant). Commercial end users should confirm end-user status in writing. Secondary sources — [Pulse Accountants](https://pulse-accountants.co.uk/blog/vat-domestic-reverse-charge-a-guide); [Zmartly: end user](https://zmartly.co.uk/news-insights/reverse-charge-end-user-construction); [BDO](https://www.bdo.co.uk/en-gb/insights/tax/vat-and-indirect-taxes/domestic-reverse-charge-vat-for-construction-services)
- So: Level 1 (commercial work, subcontractors) handles DRC both ways; Level 2 mostly receives DRC invoices from VAT-registered subbies but charges homeowners normal VAT; Level 3 issues DRC invoices when working as a VAT-registered sub to a builder.

**Contracts, quotes and consumer law (all levels with homeowner clients)**
- Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013: 14-day cancellation for most off-premises contracts. FMB: a quote given at the consumer's home without agreeing the contract there is treated as on-premises; urgent repairs requested by the consumer are excluded. — [Designing Buildings: cooling-off period](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Cooling%20off%20period); [FMB guidance PDF](https://fmb.org.uk/asset/859D7C64%2D1956%2D41D1%2D981B87FB93D9EC45); [Business Companion: off-premises](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/consumer-contracts/consumer-contracts-off-premises-sales); [BIS guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/429300/bis-13-1368-consumer-contracts-information-cancellation-and-additional-payments-regulations-guidance.pdf)
- Construction Act (HGCRA) payment and adjudication rules (payment notices, pay less notices, statutory adjudication) do not apply to contracts with a residential occupier (s.106). Court of Appeal in RBH Building Contractors Ltd v James [2026] EWCA Civ 511 gave the first appellate ruling on the exception: it can apply where intention to occupy is credibly evidenced; also said pay less notices should not become "tick-box exercises, or traps". Advice: be clear at contract stage whether the Act applies. — [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/court-of-appeal-guidance-on-pay-less-notices-and-first-ruling-on-the-residential-occupier-exception.html); [Charles Russell Speechlys](https://www.charlesrussellspeechlys.com/en/insights/expert-insights/construction-engineering-and-projects/2025/adjudication-under-the-construction-act-a-case-on-the-residential-occupier-exception-and-contesting-the-validity-of-a-payless-notice/); [Ashfords](https://www.ashfords.co.uk/insights/articles/residential-occupiers-and-pay-less-notices-key-lessons-from-recent-construction-law-judgment)
- Inference: for Level 1 commercial jobs and for any subcontract, payment applications, payment notices and pay less notices are statutory; for homeowner jobs they are contractual good practice only.

**Building control and competent person schemes (all levels; key for Level 3)**
- Competent person schemes let installers self-certify certain work instead of using building control. Covered: electrical work in dwellings; replacement windows/doors/rooflights (FENSA, CERTASS, etc.); heating and hot water, plumbing, ventilation, insulation, roof coverings, microgeneration and more; gas appliances via Gas Safe Register (not technically a CPS but similar duties). Joining needs proof of technical competence. — [gov.uk: competent person schemes](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/competent-person-scheme-current-schemes-and-how-schemes-are-authorised)
- **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]** The installer (via the scheme) notifies the local authority within 30 days of completion, and the homeowner gets a building regulations compliance certificate (commonly within 8 weeks). Electricians also issue BS 7671 certificates (EIC / Minor Works). Gas engineers notify via Gas Safe and leave a commissioning record. Not confirmed on the gov.uk page fetched.
- Work outside a CPS (extensions, lofts, structural alterations) needs a building control application (full plans or building notice) and inspections, leading to a completion certificate. **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]** — this is the paperwork that a Level 1 or 2 firm (or the client's designer) must drive.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Since 2023 the Building Safety Act regime added "dutyholder" competence duties (Building Regulations etc. (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2023) for all building work, mirroring CDM roles (principal designer / principal contractor for building regulations). I did not fetch a source in this session.

**Accreditation (CHAS, TrustMark, FENSA)**
- TrustMark application references an Insurance Backed Guarantee and public and employers' liability insurance; the page found was for FENSA-registered installers joining TrustMark. — [FENSA/TrustMark apply page](https://forms.fensa.org.uk/trustmark/apply-to-join)
- TrustMark calls itself "the Government Endorsed Quality Scheme" with vetted businesses. Detailed rules (Code of Practice, Customer Charter, deposit protection) could not be fetched (404). — [trustmark.org.uk](https://www.trustmark.org.uk/)
- CHAS/CHAS Elite pages returned 404. **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]**: CHAS is an SSIP health and safety pre-qualification scheme; CHAS Elite adds the Common Assessment Standard (PAS 91-based: H&S, environment, quality, equality, modern slavery, financial standing), renewed annually, often demanded by commercial and public-sector clients. This explains why a Level 1 firm doing commercial work shows CHAS Elite.

**Insurance**
- **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]** Employers' liability insurance is a legal requirement once a business has employees (Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969), with at least £5m cover; public liability is not legally required but is expected by clients and required by TrustMark/Checkatrade-type schemes. Relevant to Level 2 and Level 1 (employees) but not usually to a sole trader with no staff.

**Client records and GDPR**
- No source fetched this session. **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]**: all levels hold personal data (names, addresses, photos of homes), so UK GDPR applies; most must pay the ICO data protection fee unless exempt.

**Tax admin (sole traders and small builders)**
- **[UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]** Making Tax Digital for Income Tax started April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 (quarterly digital updates), dropping to £30,000 in April 2027. Highly relevant to Level 3 and unincorporated Level 2 businesses. Not checked against gov.uk in this session.

## Mandatory by law vs good practice vs accreditation

### Takeaway
Legally mandatory: CIS (where contractor-subcontractor payments exist), VAT/DRC rules (if VAT-registered), CDM duties (passed to builders on domestic jobs), building regulations sign-off (via building control or a competent person scheme), consumer cancellation information for off-premises contracts, HGCRA payment rules for non-residential/subcontract work. Accreditation-driven: CHAS/CHAS Elite (commercial pre-qualification), TrustMark (IBG, insurance), FENSA/Gas Safe (also the legal route for self-certifying). Good practice: written variations, snagging lists, handover packs, photos.

### Cited Findings
- CDM client duties transfer to contractors on domestic jobs — [HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm)
- CIS registration and monthly returns are legal duties with penalties — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor)
- DRC applies only between VAT-registered businesses within CIS — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-domestic-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services)
- HGCRA payment and adjudication provisions do not apply to residential occupiers — [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/court-of-appeal-guidance-on-pay-less-notices-and-first-ruling-on-the-residential-occupier-exception.html)
- Competent person schemes are the legal self-certification route for notifiable work — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/competent-person-scheme-current-schemes-and-how-schemes-are-authorised)
- TrustMark entry involves IBG and liability insurance — [FENSA/TrustMark](https://forms.fensa.org.uk/trustmark/apply-to-join)
- 14-day cancellation for off-premises consumer contracts — [Business Companion](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/consumer-contracts/consumer-contracts-off-premises-sales)

### Inferences
- The domestic-client CDM rule is a likely under-met legal duty for Level 2 builders: they become legally responsible for client duties and the construction phase plan, often without knowing. This is a good AI-assistant wedge (generate a proportionate plan from a job description), but needs careful legal wording.
- Accreditation paperwork (CHAS Elite questionnaires, policies, annual renewals) is a recurring burden mainly for Level 1.

- No authoritative source on whether RAMS method statements are legally required (risk assessments are; written record required with 5+ employees under MHSWR **[UNVERIFIED]**).

## Admin time burden

### Takeaway
Surveys, mostly commissioned by software or supply firms, put UK tradespeople's admin at roughly 5 to 8 hours a week, done mostly in evenings and weekends. Treat the numbers as indicative: samples are small and sponsors sell admin tools.

### Cited Findings
- HeyBRB "UK Admin Drain Report 2026" (23 Mar 2026; 167 UK owner-operators, only 13% trades; ±7.6 pts): average 8 hours a week on repetitive admin (~384 hours a year); 77% of trades respondents do admin in evenings, nearly half at weekends; over a third spend 20+ minutes writing a single quote; invoicing and payment chasing were the most wanted to automate; 75% had tried an AI tool in the past year, 25% said AI saved time but not on the tasks they needed most, 16% found output too generic; nearly a third of trades had never tried AI. Sponsor is an AI automation consultancy. — [Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/uk-tradespeople-losing-ten-working-weeks-a-year-to-admin-they-could-automate-survey-finds/)
- Powered Now survey (2026, per search snippet): about 7 hours a week on paperwork, up to 48 days a year. **[UNVERIFIED — snippet only; Powered Now sells trade job-management software]** — [Electrical Contracting News](https://electricalcontractingnews.com/news/industry-news/powered-now-tradespeople-report-growing-business-pressures/)
- "NOW Report" (June 2026, 140 tradespeople, per search snippet): ~5h20m a week on admin, much in evenings/weekends; 93% report stress or anxiety from running their business. **[UNVERIFIED — snippet only]** — [Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/?p=36067)
- Parker and Chandlers Building Supplies survey of 700 builders (Apr 2021): 62% of general builders struggle to keep paperwork in order; 62% find it hard to make time for admin; two-thirds do paperwork evenings/weekends; almost half say the van doubles as an office; 70% would rather be on the tools; only 6% pay someone to do most admin. — [Builders Merchants Journal](https://buildersmerchantsjournal.net/paperwork-is-builders-biggest-stress-merchant-research-finds/)
- Dakea survey of 500 tradespeople: financial admin issues (lost receipts, expenses) cost UK tradespeople over £929m a year. **[UNVERIFIED — snippet only]** — [Building Construction Design](https://buildingconstructiondesign.co.uk/?p=260269)
- Painting and decorating: "admin overload could cost painting and decorating professionals almost £10,000 a year" (headline only). **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Painting and Decorating News](https://www.paintinganddecoratingnews.co.uk/uncategorized/admin-overload-could-cost-painting-and-decorating-professionals-almost-10000-a-year-survey-finds/)
- Sage blog cites 13.9 hours a week from an older report; source not traceable, treat as outlier. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Sage](https://www.sage.com/en-gb/blog/tradespeople-save-time-improve-efficiency/)
- Screwfix "Voice of the Trade" manifesto 2025 exists as a possible source (not read). — [Screwfix PDF](https://media.screwfix.com/is/content/ae235/PDF_Download/voice_of_the_trade_manifesto_2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Consistent signals: admin is done out of hours, in the van, and quoting/invoicing/chasing are the top pain tasks. A phone-first or voice-first assistant fits how they work.
- The HeyBRB finding that generic AI output disappoints suggests value comes from trade-specific templates (CIS-aware invoices, CDM-aware plans, trade-specific quotes).

### Gaps
- No FMB, FSB, CITB or Checkatrade primary figures on hours per week found in this session. No data split by the three levels (design-and-build vs crew vs sole trader).

## Main pain points

### Takeaway
Late payment, rising costs and insolvency pressure, skills shortages, quoting time and admin stress dominate. Late payment is improving slightly but 4 in 10 SME builders still report variable or late payment.

### Cited Findings
- FMB/CIOB State of Trade H2 2025 (survey 26 Jan-20 Feb 2026, 493 UK SME construction firms): 57% say invoices paid within agreed terms, 29% variable timelines, 13% frequent late payment; report highlights rising costs, insolvencies and skills shortages; FMB's Brian Berry called for tackling late payment and affordable apprentice hiring. — [FMB press release](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/construction-smes-face-mounting-cost-pressures-and-insolvencies-despite-continuous-growth-report-warns.html); [CIOB](https://www.ciob.org/news/construction-smes-face-mounting-cost-pressures-and-insolvencies-despite-continuous-growth-report-warns)
- FMB/CIOB H1 2025 (458 responses, pub. Sept 2025); Scotland: 49% paid on time, 39% variable, 10% often late. — [FMB Scotland](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/scottish-construction-recovery-builds-momentum-but-homeowners-face-delays-and-rising-costs-warn-industry-bodies.html)
- Half of construction SMEs reporting losses (headline). **[UNVERIFIED — headline only]** — [Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/half-of-construction-smes-are-reporting-losses-new-survey-shows)
- Quoting time and payment chasing: over a third spend 20+ minutes per quote from scratch; frequent chasing of overdue payments. — [Electrical Times / HeyBRB](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/uk-tradespeople-losing-ten-working-weeks-a-year-to-admin-they-could-automate-survey-finds/)
- Stress: 93% stressed by running the business (NOW Report, snippet) **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/?p=36067); paperwork named builders' biggest stress (2021) — [Builders Merchants Journal](https://buildersmerchantsjournal.net/paperwork-is-builders-biggest-stress-merchant-research-finds/)
- Disputes: homeowners cannot be forced into statutory adjudication (s.106), so domestic disputes go to negotiation or court; pay less notice validity is litigated. — [Ashfords](https://www.ashfords.co.uk/insights/articles/residential-occupiers-and-pay-less-notices-key-lessons-from-recent-construction-law-judgment)

### Inferences
- For homeowner work, builders lack the Construction Act's payment safety net, so clear written quotes, staged payment terms and written variations are the main protection against late payment and disputes. An AI assistant that turns a site walk-round or voice note into a priced quote, and logs variations with client sign-off, targets the top two pain points directly.
- Level 1 pain points tilt to compliance (CHAS Elite, CDM, building control coordination, handover packs); Level 3 to quoting, invoicing, certificates and getting paid.

### Gaps
- No primary data on "finding work" or dispute frequency (Checkatrade, TrustMark complaint statistics not found). No Federation of Small Businesses construction-specific late-payment figure found.
