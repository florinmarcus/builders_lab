# Small and medium UK design-and-build firms (high-end refurbishment and new homes): procedures and software

All sources checked on 19 Nov 2026 unless a line says otherwise. Example firm: Robert's 21st Century Design (Hatfield, Hertfordshire). No personal data collected: firms and public business roles only. "Evidence" = stated by a source. "Inference" = my reading, not stated by a source.

Overall caution: most of what firms say in public is marketing. Few small firms name their software in public. Job ads and vendor case studies give better clues, but small firms rarely post detailed ads, and the main vendors (Buildertrend, Houzz Pro) publish mostly US case studies.

## Procedures: client journey, RIBA stages, CDM, CHAS Elite, warranty, client communication

### Takeaway
Firms describe a single-team journey from concept, through planning, to build and interiors, with one firm managing every stage. The regulatory paperwork is real and heavy: CDM duties fall on the contractor on domestic jobs, CHAS Elite needs documented evidence across 13 risk areas, and structural warranties (LABC Warranty, NHBC) need application forms, plans, building control details, stage inspections and completion documents. Communication with clients often runs over phone and WhatsApp, and UK courts have treated WhatsApp messages as binding contracts, which makes variations recorded in chats a legal risk.

Services named: architectural design, planning approval, construction and interior design. It will "work closely with you from the initial concept to the final touches" and "manage every stage". — [roberts21stcenturydesign.com](https://www.roberts21stcenturydesign.com/)
- Shows CHAS and CHAS Elite logos and Best of Houzz awards (Design 2019–2023, Service 2016, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2024). It mentions no client portal, 3D tool, app, warranty provider or trade body. It says it partners with "experienced architects, builders, and interior designers". Social channels linked: Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn, TikTok. — [roberts21stcenturydesign.com](https://www.roberts21stcenturydesign.com/)

**How the build is managed (similar firms)**
- HollandGreen (architecture and design practice, Cotswolds, Bucks, Oxon, London, Berks) names three build roles: the main contractor's Site Manager (manpower, materials, timelines), a Contracts Administrator or QS who "tracks and approves variations" and authorises client payments against deliverables, and its own project management team that coordinates clients, contractors and consultants. — [HollandGreen FAQ: How is the build managed](https://www.hollandgreen.co.uk/faq/how-is-the-build-managed/)
- Hampstead Renovations (London NW3, also serves parts of Herts, Surrey, Bucks) promotes one accountable team "from first survey to final snagging", with scope, programme and budget agreed before work starts. — [Hampstead Renovations, central London page](https://www.hampsteadrenovations.co.uk/property-refurbishment-central-london/) (from search snippet, page not fetched)
- Benhams: dedicated project managers; overseas clients get updates by Zoom or video call. — [Benhams refurbishment](https://www.benhams.com/services/refurbishment/) (search snippet)

**CDM 2015 on domestic jobs (evidence, HSE)**
- Domestic client duties do not disappear: on a single-contractor job they pass to the contractor; on a multi-contractor job to the principal contractor. — [HSE: Domestic clients](https://www.hse.gov.uk/Construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm)
- A domestic client can instead ask a designer to take on the client duties as principal designer, but only with a written agreement; without it, duties go to the principal contractor. — [HSE: Domestic clients](https://www.hse.gov.uk/Construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm); [HSE: Are you a domestic client?](https://hse.gov.uk/construction/areyou/client.htm)
- Job ads for residential extension/refurbishment PMs list CDM set-up duties such as RAMS and construction phase plans, with 6–8 projects run at once. — [SimplyHired job listing](https://www.simplyhired.co.uk/job/b-ZKozQV6tTfSfmtkXqEJ2MhRpgTJr874hcSXbFqukpA1P5l0MuwIw) (search snippet; listing may have closed)

**CHAS Elite / SSIP evidence (CHAS is the seller, treat as one provider's view)**
- CHAS Elite is based on the Common Assessment Standard and covers 13 areas of risk management. — [CHAS: Common Assessment Standard / Elite](https://www.chas.co.uk/contractors/common-assessment-standard/)
- Answering the question set is "just the first step"; firms must upload documents as evidence, and assessors want proof that policies and procedures are "actively implemented, maintained, and reviewed". Holding a valid SSIP certificate removes questions already answered. — [CHAS: What is the CAS](https://www.chas.co.uk/blog/what-is-the-common-assessment-standard/); [CHAS: why certification is hard to evidence](https://www.chas.co.uk/blog/why-contractor-safety-certification-can-be-difficult-to-evidence-under-the-common-assessment-standard/)
- Common problems CHAS names: inconsistent records, outdated documents, collecting evidence across several sites, no proof processes are followed. — [CHAS: why UK contractors struggle with CAS](https://www.chas.co.uk/blog/why-uk-contractors-struggle-with-common-assessment-standard-and-ssip-compliance/)

**Structural warranty paperwork (new homes and conversions)**
- LABC Warranty application asks: new build, conversion or refurbishment; for conversions, existing and proposed plans; site address, start/end dates, whether works started; building control body and date of Building Regulations submission. It only quotes when a local authority or the BSR does building control. — [LABC Warranty application form](https://www.labcwarranty.co.uk/application-form); [Information needed](https://info.labcwarranty.co.uk/information-needed-to-complete-your-application-form?hsLang=en)
- First-time developer/builder 12-month registration fee: £750, non-refundable. — [LABC Warranty registration FAQ](https://www.labcwarranty.co.uk/faqs/warranty-registration) (via search summary)
- Inspections: initial assessment, stage inspections, final inspection, plus extra for basements and roofs. For conversions, surveyors must see the existing building before it is covered up. Before final inspection: close open conditions, finish engineering/design/technical reviews, upload completion documents to the LABC Warranty extranet. — [LABC Warranty inspections FAQ](https://www.labcwarranty.co.uk/faqs/warranty-inspections-and-processes); [LABCW final inspection PDF](https://www.labcwarranty.co.uk/hubfs/Blog/Blog%20PDFs/LABCW%20How%20do%20I%20achieve%20a%20successful%20final%20inspection%20.pdf)
- NHBC: builder registers, then registers each plot for Buildmark cover (cost depends on premium rating and build cost); inspectors check at key stages against NHBC Standards. — [NHBC warranty FAQs](https://www.nhbc.co.uk/warranties/frequently-asked-questions)

**Client communication and WhatsApp**
- Hampstead Renovations has a "Chat with us on WhatsApp" button next to phone and enquiry form. — [Hampstead Renovations 3D visualisation](https://www.hampsteadrenovations.co.uk/3d-visualisation)
- In Jaevee Homes v Fincham (2025) a series of WhatsApp messages was held to be a binding construction contract. — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/whatsapp-construction-contract-held-be-binding-english-law); [Kennedys](https://kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/case-review/2025/the-whatsapp-contract-jaevee-homes-ltd-v-fincham)
- Site WhatsApp groups can blur who the contracting parties are, messages can be lost or deleted, and they may need disclosure in a dispute. — [Construction Management: Should we use WhatsApp for site communications?](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/should-we-use-whatsapp-for-site-communications/)
- Advice for variations: confirm every change in writing and keep a running change log; agreements often happen by WhatsApp or text in practice. — [Willans: building disputes advice](https://www.willans.co.uk/knowledge/building-disputes-advice/); [Renno: how to handle variations](https://www.renno.pro/blog/how-to-handle-variations-in-construction)

### Inferences
- Typical journey for a firm like Robert's (inference from the firm sites above): enquiry → survey and brief → concept design (often 3D) → planning application → building regulations / building control → detailed design and pricing → contract → build (site manager, CDM paperwork) → variations → snagging → handover with completion documents (warranty, building control completion, O&M) → aftercare.
- A firm doing both new builds and refurbishments with CHAS Elite will hold a large, repeating set of documents: RAMS, construction phase plans, insurance certificates, training records, warranty forms, building control approvals. Good RAG content and a clear demo (find a document, check expiry, draft a RAMS from a template).
- Because Robert's partners with outside architects and designers, it likely receives drawings by email or shared folder rather than drawing in-house (inference, not stated).
- The WhatsApp legal risk is a strong demo point: an assistant that turns a WhatsApp/email approval into a written variation record with price and date.

## Software typically used by such firms in the UK

### Takeoff
Hard evidence on the software stack of small UK design-and-build firms is thin. What the evidence shows: Procore and MS Project/Asta appear in ads for larger high-end contractors; Houzz Pro is promoted to UK renovation firms (estimates, takeoffs, client management); design-and-build firms use drafting/rendering software much less than architects (36% vs 76%); AI is used mostly for admin. Buildertrend had no UK case study I could find. Accounting (Xero, QuickBooks) is covered in the sibling note `research/integrations/accounting-apis.md`.

### Cited Findings
**Surveys**
- Houzz 2025 UK State of the Industry (N=160 Houzz UK firms, fieldwork Dec 2024–Feb 2025, published 25 Mar 2025): drafting and rendering software used by 76% of architects, 69% of interior designers and 36% of design and build firms. Firms also use software for billing and invoicing, marketing, CRM, time tracking and websites (no percentages). 87% plan to invest: 42% in efficiency, 37% in the sales process, 35% in client and team communication. — [InsightDIY summary](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/houzz-publishes-2025-uk-state-of-the-industry-report/14904.htm)
- Houzz UK State of AI in Construction and Design (over 145 pros, article 14 May 2026): 46% use AI; uses: admin 79% (89% of construction pros), sales and marketing 52%, planning and design 33%, project and client management 31%. Barriers: reliability, data security and privacy, lack of training. Average time saved "over 3 hours per week" (Houzz claim). — [KBB Focus](https://kbbfocus.com/news/6084-new-houzz-report-ai-adoption-grows-across-uk-construction-and-design)
- FMB/CIOB State of Trade H1 2026 (416 SME responses, Jan–Jun 2026, run by Barbour ABI, page dated 10 Nov 2026): 57% use AI more than a year ago, 22% do not use it at all. Of AI users: 80% for office and admin tasks, 42% for quotes, estimates and tenders, 33% for health, safety and quality management. — [FMB State of Trade H1 2026](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-h1-2026-skills-rising-costs-and-ai-adoption.html)
- NBS Digital Construction Report 2025 (559 design and construction professionals, published 15 Oct 2025): more than two in five use AI tools; BIM and cloud collaboration described as largely normal practice. No small-firm split found. — [NBS press release](https://www.thenbs.com/news-and-press/ai-adoption-surges-as-digital-anxiety-grips-the-construction-sector); [NBS report page](https://www.thenbs.com/digital-construction-report-2025)
- FMB Master Builder (Spring 2026) cites PlanRadar research: 97% of UK construction professionals expect more digital investment in the next three years, especially project management software (vendor research). — [Master Builder Spring 2026](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/436411427/26/) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Likely stack for a 10–50 person high-end design-and-build firm (inference, not evidence): Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace (email, OneDrive/SharePoint or Google Drive/Dropbox for drawings and photos), Xero or QuickBooks or Sage for accounts, WhatsApp for site and client chat, spreadsheets for estimates and change logs, and maybe one construction PM tool (Houzz Pro for Houzz-active firms; Procore only near the £2m+ project end). Robert's Houzz awards make Houzz Pro a plausible candidate, but this is not confirmed.
- Best MCP integration points that already exist or are cheap: Gmail/Outlook, Google Drive/OneDrive, Xero (official MCP, see accounting note), Google Calendar. Construction PM tools (Houzz Pro, Buildertrend) have no public MCP found here; Procore has an API but suits larger firms.
- Demo scenarios matching survey evidence (AI used mostly for admin and estimates): draft a variation from a WhatsApp/email thread; chase overdue invoices in Xero; assemble a CHAS evidence pack from Drive; prepare a warranty or handover pack checklist.

## Where the biggest admin pains sit

### Takeaway
Evidence points to admin and paperwork in general (80% of AI-using SMEs use it for admin), estimating and tendering (days per takeoff, several tenders a week), health and safety evidence (CHAS), variations recorded in informal chats, and warranty/handover documents. Data on planning and building control correspondence pain was not found.

### Cited Findings
- 80% of AI-using SME builders use it for office and admin, 42% for quotes/estimates/tenders, 33% for H&S and quality. — [FMB H1 2026](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-h1-2026-skills-rising-costs-and-ai-adoption.html)
- Houzz UK: 89% of construction pros using AI use it for admin; 35% of firms plan investment in client and team communication, 37% in the sales process. — [KBB Focus](https://kbbfocus.com/news/6084-new-houzz-report-ai-adoption-grows-across-uk-construction-and-design); [InsightDIY](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/houzz-publishes-2025-uk-state-of-the-industry-report/14904.htm)
- Profit pressure: 62% of Houzz UK firms saw lower profitability in 2024, 47% lower revenue. — [InsightDIY](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/houzz-publishes-2025-uk-state-of-the-industry-report/14904.htm)
- Takeoffs by hand took 2–3 days; firm tenders 2–3 times a week. — [Houzz Pro Learn](https://pro.houzz.co.uk/pro-learn/blog/josh-fortnum-on-how-clever-tech-solutions-help-him) (vendor story)
- CHAS evidence pains: inconsistent records, out-of-date documents, evidence across sites. — [CHAS](https://www.chas.co.uk/blog/why-uk-contractors-struggle-with-common-assessment-standard-and-ssip-compliance/)
- Variations agreed by WhatsApp/text can bind and are hard to prove; advice is a written change log. — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/whatsapp-construction-contract-held-be-binding-english-law); [Renno](https://www.renno.pro/blog/how-to-handle-variations-in-construction)
- Warranty final inspection needs closed conditions and uploaded completion documents. — [LABCW final inspection PDF](https://www.labcwarranty.co.uk/hubfs/Blog/Blog%20PDFs/LABCW%20How%20do%20I%20achieve%20a%20successful%20final%20inspection%20.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ranking for demo choice (inference): 1) variations and client approvals from WhatsApp/email; 2) estimates and quotes; 3) compliance and evidence packs (CHAS, RAMS); 4) handover pack (warranty, building control completion, manuals); 5) procurement (orders, long-lead items; Ressi orders early and takes deposits for long-lead items, per the Houzz story).
- Planning and building control correspondence is likely a pain for firms that handle planning in-house (Robert's lists "planning approval"), but no source measures it.

### Gaps
- No UK survey measuring time lost to variations, approvals or handover in small firms.
- No source on planning and building control correspondence burden.
- Procurement pain is supported only by one vendor story.
