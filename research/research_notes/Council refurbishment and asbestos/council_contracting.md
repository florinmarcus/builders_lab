# How English councils and social landlords contract small firms to refurbish council flats (as of Nov 2026)

Scope: England. Researched 7 Nov 2026. Every claim has a link. "UNVERIFIED" marks items from memory or secondary sources not checked against a primary page. No personal data.

Method note: about 20 searches and fetches. Primary documents read in full: Runnymede Borough Council Void Standard Policy 2022-25 (PDF), Procurement for Housing (PfH) Responsive Repairs, Voids and Associated Works DPS brochure (PDF), GOV.UK Awaab's Law timeframes guidance. Everything else comes from search snippets of council reports, framework notices and law-firm commentary.

## 1. Types of work and which ones small firms get

### Takeaway
Social landlord property work splits into voids (empty homes made ready to re-let), responsive repairs, planned works (kitchens, bathrooms, Decent Homes) and adaptations. Voids and adaptations come up again and again as lots and DPS categories open to small, regional and single-trade firms. Large responsive-repairs-plus-voids contracts tend to go to big main contractors.

### Cited Findings
- The PfH Responsive Repairs, Voids & Associated Works DPS has five lots: (1) full-service/multi-discipline, (2) general repairs and joinery, (3) plumbing and heating, (4) electrical, (5) specialist works. Each lot covers "responsive repairs requirements and / or completion of ... voids refurbishment works". Contractors "may be required to supplement existing direct labour" or manage the whole process, including call handling and scheduling. Minimum requirements to join: Gas Safe and NICEIC for lot 1, Gas Safe for lot 3, NICEIC or equivalent for lot 4. Everything else is "as defined by customer at ITT stage". — [PfH DPS brochure (PDF)](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2630870_DPSResponsiveRepairsVoidsWorks-PfH_082225.pdf)
- PfH described the DPS as worth about £490m and as a way to support small, local firms through a simpler tender process (search snippet). — [Glass Times](https://glasstimes.co.uk/news/glazing-contractors-needed/)
- The East of England EEM DPS includes "void property maintenance including works to bring properties back to re-let standards" (search snippet). — [Contracts Finder notice](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/8fa9376f-3e88-479f-a3ec-f3debc52851b)
- Doncaster Borough Council set up a DPS for housing adaptation works funded by Disabled Facilities Grants. — [Stotles notice](https://www.stotles.com/explore/notices/59150b15-1257-421d-a2f0-b10d39252de0/dynamic-purchasing-system-for-the-provision-of-housing-adaptation-works)
- Large contracts bundle everything. For example, Mears is to deliver "responsive repairs, voids, gas servicing, heating installations and planned maintenance" under a 10-year, £450m contract at Birmingham City Council (article date unclear). — [Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/?p=510467)
- Hammersmith & Fulham ran a separate procurement for "Disrepair and Complex Works" (title only seen). — [LBHF committee report](https://democracy.lbhf.gov.uk/documents/s132825/Procurement%20Strategy%20And%20Contract%20Award%20-%20Disrepair%20And%20Complex%20Works.pdf)
- Under the Runnymede void standard, gas work is not done by the voids contractor: "All gas works are to be carried out by RBC's Specialist contractor". The voids contractor liaises with that contractor. — [Runnymede Void Standard (PDF)](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)

### Inferences
- A small multi-trade firm without its own Gas Safe engineer fits best into void refurbishment (general building, joinery, decoration, kitchens and bathrooms, cleaning, clearance), single-trade lots, adaptations, or second-tier work under a main contractor. Gas is often split out to a specialist.
- Void work is batchy and property-centred: one address, one set of keys, one specification. That suits a per-property document assistant well.

### Gaps
- I found no source that measures what share of void or repair work goes to SMEs or to Romanian-run firms. The idea that these firms are "often Romanian-run" is the brief's premise, not a sourced fact.
- Planned works (kitchen and bathroom programmes) were not researched in depth.

## 2. How small firms get the work

### Takeaway
There are four main routes. (a) DPSs, which firms can join at any time (PfH, regional consortia). (b) Frameworks with regional and value-band lots (PfH, Fusion21, LHC). (c) A council's own tenders and below-threshold quotes. (d) Subcontracting to a big main contractor (Mears and similar). The Procurement Act 2023 replaced the old rules for new procurements, but existing DPSs keep running until Oct 2028 at the latest.

### Cited Findings
- A DPS lets new suppliers "apply to join at any point during its lifetime". Councils can work with their own specifications, pricing and contract terms, and "your requirement can be tendered in as little as 10 days". The PfH DPS was set up under the Public Contracts Regulations 2015. — [PfH DPS brochure (PDF)](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2630870_DPSResponsiveRepairsVoidsWorks-PfH_082225.pdf)
- LHC says DPSs suit small and medium contractors (blog title). — [LHC blog](https://lse.lhcprocure.org.uk/news/blogs/why-small-and-medium-sized-contractors-should-consider-a-dps)
- Under the Procurement Act 2023, existing DPSs can continue, but transitional rules say they must expire by 27 Oct 2028 at the latest. The Act does not allow below-threshold contracts to be awarded through the new "dynamic markets". A DPS can still be used for a below-threshold award if there is no separate qualification stage. — [Bevan Brittan](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2024/can-dynamic-purchasing-systems-be-used-for-below-threshold-contracts/)
- Below-threshold rules are in Part 6 of the Act (ss. 84-88). A suitability assessment for regulated below-threshold contracts is banned by s.85(1). — [Bevan Brittan](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2024/can-dynamic-purchasing-systems-be-used-for-below-threshold-contracts/); [Procurement Act 2023, legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54)
- PfH's Responsive Housing Repairs and Void Property Services framework named 75 firms. It is worth £725m over three years, has regional lots (five suppliers per region on one lot), value bands up to £1.5m, and runs to 31 Jul 2027. — [PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/building-control-news/75-contractors-appointed-to-pfhs-reactive-repairs-framework/164349/); [PfH framework PDF](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2614082_FrameworkResponsiveRepairsVoidsAssociatedWorks_PfH_061125.pdf)
- Fusion21 invited bids for a £350m Responsive Repairs and Void Property framework with a housing refurbishment lot split by value band and region (search snippet). — [Fusion21 Housing Lot 1a](https://www.fusion21.co.uk/lot/housing-lot-1a)
- LHC H3 (2026) is a £1bn housing, regeneration and demolition framework with 12 lots. For the first time it reserves up to three places for micro or small firms on some lots, including low-value retrofit. It is not a voids framework. — [Construction Enquirer, 1 May 2026](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/05/01/lhc-launches-1bn-housing-and-demolition-framework/)
- LHC RM3 (refurbishment, successor to WH1/WH2) splits lots by region with up to eight bidders per area. — [Find a Tender 021999-2025](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/021999-2025)
- LHC announced a £360m public sector refurbishment framework that "creates opportunities for SMEs" (title). — [LHC news](https://www.lhcprocure.org.uk/news/news/new-360-million-public-sector-refurbishment-framework-creates-opportunities-for-smes)
- Section 93 of the Act requires a pipeline notice for contracts over £2m. — [DLA Piper](https://dlapiperintelligence.com/procurement-act)
- Under the Act, s.68 implies 30-day payment terms into public contracts and s.73 carries them down to public sub-contracts. — [Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/the-evolution-of-sub-contracting-under-the-procurement-act-2023); [Ward Hadaway](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/procurement-in-a-nutshell/procurement-in-a-nutshell-supply-chain-payment-terms/)

### Inferences
- A small firm's realistic entry points are joining a DPS lot for its region, bidding for council quotes under the threshold (published on Contracts Finder or the council's e-tendering portal), or getting onto a main contractor's supply chain.
- Every route produces a mix of channels: portal messages from the framework or DPS, then the council's own email and portal for each job.

### Gaps
- Current threshold figures (updated from Jan 2026) were not checked on GOV.UK. UNVERIFIED: the works threshold has been around £5.4m (incl. VAT) since 2024.
- I found no source on how Mears, Wates, Morgan Sindall or Equans recruit and manage voids subcontractors (terms, rates, onboarding platforms such as Constructionline or CHAS). The search returned only general material.
- Approved contractor lists at single councils were not documented.

## 3. How work is ordered and paid

### Takeaway
Work is ordered per property through a works order, usually priced from a schedule of rates such as M3NHF. Variations are agreed with the client. The client inspects before handover. Payment is applied for through the landlord's portal or by invoice. Public sub-contracts have 30-day payment terms implied by law. CIS deductions apply to labour.

### Cited Findings
- The M3NHF Schedule of Rates for Responsive Maintenance and Void Property Works, Version 8, was revised in 2023 by Rand Associates. Each item has a "6 character alpha numeric code" and a "single character alpha priority code". Units include per item (IT), linear metre (LM) and others. Each item covers "the entire work content of the particular repair", including all ancillary items. "No further payment is due ... beyond the payments provided for in the Schedule of Rates", adjusted by a tendered discount. The Client Representative's decision on "approved, directed or selected" is final. Works must be done within the response periods set in the contract. — [M3NHF SoR v8 extract, Sell2Wales](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/Assets/NoticeBuilder_FileDownload.aspx?id=357559) (a Welsh notice, but the same national schedule is used in England)
- Peabody's NEC Housing contractor portal lets contractors view and add notes to works orders, vary an order and record the actual cost, mark orders complete and apply for payment. Each contractor sees only its own orders (guide written for NEC Housing v22.1). — [Peabody NEC Contractor Portal guide (PDF)](https://www.peabodygroup.org.uk/media/tqylexo0/nec-contractor-portal-v211.pdf)
- Fixflo says its contractor tools apply "the right costs ... using your schedule of rates". — [Fixflo social housing](https://www.fixflo.com/solutions/social-housing)
- Runnymede void process, step by step:
  - The contractor's responsibility starts "on receipt of a functioning set of keys".
  - Before inspection and specification of works, the contractor gets pre-termination information from the Contract Administrator.
  - Serious defects and tenant rechargeable items are reported "immediately" with photos.
  - The EICR test certificate, showing Code 1 and 2 defects, is provided "before instructions are given". Remedial works follow approval.
  - There is a "maximum turnaround timescale of 15 working days", monitored as a KPI.
  - There is a joint handover meeting where the council confirms the works meet the void standard. Keys go back only after that.
  - Minor outstanding items may be accepted at handover, to be completed within 28 days of occupation.
  - The receipt date is entered on "RBC's Contractors' Portal", marking the end of the works phase.
  - There is a 12-month defects period from handover.
  — [Runnymede Void Standard (PDF)](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)
- Per-void budgets. Ashfield District Council: average void cost £2,682.38 in 2024/25 against a £1,950 budget per property, which had not risen since 2018. The figure was expected to rise because of "missing contractor invoices". — [Ashfield scrutiny report, Mar 2025](https://www.ashfield.gov.uk/media/dhhbew21/scrutiny-findings-report-march-2025.docx)
- Payment terms. Section 68(2) of the Procurement Act 2023: pay within 30 days of receiving the invoice. Section 68(6): this cannot be overridden. It does not apply to invalid or disputed invoices (s.68(3)). Section 73 implies the same terms into public sub-contracts. Authorities publish six-monthly payments compliance notices. — [Ward Hadaway](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/procurement-in-a-nutshell/procurement-in-a-nutshell-supply-chain-payment-terms/); [GOV.UK guidance: payments compliance notices](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-manage-phase/guidance-payments-compliance-notices-html); [legislation.gov.uk Part 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/part/4/crossheading/terms-implied-into-public-contracts)
- CIS deductions are 20% for registered subcontractors, 30% for unregistered ones and 0% with gross payment status. They apply to labour, not materials, VAT or the CITB levy. — [GOV.UK CIS contractor guide](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor/make-deductions-and-pay-subcontractors); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-in/knowledge/publications/ae7499c6/the-uk-construction-industry-scheme)

### Inferences
- Each property's paper trail looks something like this: works order and SOR lines, then pre-inspection and specification, then variation requests and approvals, then mid-works reports (defects, rechargeables, EICR results), then completion and handover pack, then invoice or payment application, then query or dispute emails. Variations and post-inspection disputes are where a searchable per-property history is most valuable.
- "Missing contractor invoices" (Ashfield) suggests the invoice-to-job link breaks on the council side too.

### Gaps
- UNVERIFIED: whether councils act as CIS "deemed contractors" (HMRC applies a construction spend test to public bodies). Not checked on GOV.UK.
- I found no source describing post-inspection sampling rates (for example, the percentage of jobs inspected) or how valuation disputes are settled.
- "Batch budgets per group of properties" were not confirmed in any source.

## 4. Documents per property, both ways

### Takeaway
Going to the contractor: keys, works order or specification against the void standard, pre-termination property information (gas, electrical and EPC status, asbestos information, component ages), the asbestos survey or register, and for occupied homes, tenant contact and access details. Coming back from the contractor: meter photos, defect and rechargeable photos, EICR then EIC, gas safety certificate (where it applies), EPC, a completed handover schedule, and an invoice.

### Cited Findings
- To the contractor (Runnymede):
  - "general property information, such as status of electrical, gas and energy efficiency certification, any asbestos information, and predicted remaining lives or replacement dates of main components"
  - the asbestos register, which the contractor must check and brief operatives on
  - keys
  — [Runnymede Void Standard (PDF)](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)
- From the contractor (Runnymede):
  - photos of gas, electric and water meter readings at start and handover
  - photos of the stop tap and meter positions
  - photos of rechargeable items and of fixtures left in good condition
  - immediate reports of serious defects (structural, damp)
  - referral of installed aids and adaptations for a decision
  - an EICR with Code 1 and 2 defects, then an electrical installation certificate
  - an EPC "at Handover"
  - a completed handover schedule (meter readings, property attributes, remaining lives of key components, decoration assessment, certification details, outstanding items)
  - a landlord's gas safety certificate where it applies
  - at least 2 sets of keys per lock, plus window keys
  — [Runnymede Void Standard (PDF)](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)
- Hammersmith & Fulham void schedule: an asbestos survey "shall be available or undertaken ... for all properties when they become empty". Removal happens only under the council's voids asbestos policy or where the survey directs it. The contractor provides enabling works for the survey and any removal or encapsulation. — [LBHF Appendix 2 Void schedule (PDF)](https://democracy.lbhf.gov.uk/documents/s123732/Appendix%202%20-%20Void%20schedule.pdf)
- Tendring (2025 asbestos policy): "Copies of Asbestos Surveys will be sent to contractors or access provided to the council's online survey data base". The policy includes a contractor review of the register and a permit-to-work form. — [Tendring Asbestos Management Policy 2025 (PDF)](https://tdcdemocracy.tendringdc.gov.uk/documents/s73633/A5%20Appendix%20B%20-%20Asbestos%20Management%20Policy%202025.pdf)
- Lambeth publishes a Housing Asbestos Management Procedure (2024), and Waltham Forest an asbestos guidance note for contractors (titles only; not read). — [Lambeth (PDF)](https://www.lambeth.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-07/Housing_Asbestos_Management-Procedure_24.pdf); [Waltham Forest (PDF)](https://thehub-beta.walthamforest.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2020-02/asbestos_guidance_note_4_-_contractors.pdf)
- A Kensington and Chelsea asbestos DPIA lists the data repair contractors receive: tenant contact details, property addresses, appointment and access information, and vulnerability indicators where needed (search snippet). — [RBKC DPIA (PDF)](https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/Committees/documents/s36760/ED5014342R3%20DPIA.pdf)
- Awaab's Law guidance expects landlords to keep photo or sample evidence before removing mould, to keep records of access attempts and contractor correspondence, and expects contractors to "supply information for written summaries promptly" and report other hazards they see. — [GOV.UK Awaab's Law timeframes guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector)

### Inferences
- For one property, a contractor's document set is likely to hold 10-20 items in mixed formats: PDFs (surveys, certificates, specifications), photos, spreadsheets (handover schedules, SOR pricing) and email threads. That is a good fit for per-property RAG with document-type tags (asbestos, EICR, gas, EPC, handover, variation, invoice).
- Fire risk assessments for blocks are probably shared for flats in blocks, but I did not confirm this from a source.

### Gaps
- No source found that shows damp and mould reports, or FRA extracts, as standard items sent with a void works order.
- I did not find a published contractor handbook that lists all documents end to end. Runnymede's void standard is the closest.

## 5. How the documents are sent

### Takeaway
There is a mix. The landlord's housing management system has a contractor portal (NEC Housing, Open Housing, My Home / Housing Online and others) for orders, status, completion and payment. Email with PDF attachments and links to online databases (asbestos) fill the gaps. Handover schedules and photos often travel outside the portal.

### Cited Findings
- NEC Housing contractor portal (Peabody): works order notes, variations, completion, payment applications. — [Peabody NEC guide (PDF)](https://www.peabodygroup.org.uk/media/tqylexo0/nec-contractor-portal-v211.pdf)
- City of York: contractors get "work orders, email notifications, job updates, documentation, invoices" through its Open Housing contractor portal (search snippet). — [York Open Housing](https://www.york.gov.uk/OpenHousing?docid=home)
- The My Home Portal for Contractors (Housing Online) lets contractors update, complete and cancel jobs, "upload supporting photos", mark attendance, flag no access and add notes. — [My Home for Contractors (PDF)](https://www.housing-online.com/resources/My_Home_For_Contractors_04.pdf); [webinar page](https://housing-online.com/the-my-home-contractor-portal-webinar)
- Runnymede uses a "Contractors' Portal" to record handover receipt, but meter photos and rechargeable photos are "passed to the Contract Administrator", channel not stated. — [Runnymede Void Standard (PDF)](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)
- Tendring sends asbestos surveys or gives access to an online survey database. — [Tendring policy (PDF)](https://tdcdemocracy.tendringdc.gov.uk/documents/s73633/A5%20Appendix%20B%20-%20Asbestos%20Management%20Policy%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- A small contractor working for 2-3 landlords juggles several portals and their own email inbox. Portals are landlord-owned and usually cannot be exported in bulk. Email is the one channel the contractor fully controls, which supports an email-first assistant.

### Gaps
- No primary sources found for Civica CX, Capita/OHMS, MRI, Northgate, Kirona/Job Manager, Total Mobile or Sypro contractor portals. Search returned nothing specific. Their share of the council market is unknown.
- How often councils still email PDFs rather than use portals is not measured anywhere I found.

## 6. Volumes, cost per void and turnaround

### Takeaway
Social landlord re-let times averaged around 40-50 days in 2024-25 (Housemark). Council targets range from 10 to 15 working days for standard voids up to 65 days for major ones. Average works cost per void in councils that publish it is about £2,400-£2,850. Definitions vary a lot.

### Cited Findings
- Housemark: standard void re-let times close to 50 days (against under 30 five years earlier), later falling to about 41 days. The vacancy rate was 0.43% in July 2025, a three-year low. — [Inside Housing](https://www.insidehousing.co.uk/news/social-landlords-reduce-void-properties-to-three-year-low-93915); [Housing Today](https://www.housingtoday.co.uk/news/social-landlord-voids-drop-to-three-year-low-says-new-report/5138365.article)
- Housemark estimated that void properties cost the social housing sector £1.6bn (headline). — [Scottish Housing News](https://www.scottishhousingnews.com/articles/housemark-void-properties-cost-social-housing-sector-ps16bn)
- Chesterfield (Dec 2025): average 192.28 days to turn around a void, against 30.40 for HouseMark peers. Void works cost per unit £286.45 against a median of £272.40 (the per-unit basis probably means per stock unit; not checked). — [Chesterfield scrutiny report (PDF)](https://chesterfield.gov.uk/media/e5ldsw5z/6c-tcp-scrutiny-report-voids.pdf)
- Ashfield: £2,682 average per void in 2024/25, budget £1,950. — [Ashfield scrutiny, Mar 2025](https://www.ashfield.gov.uk/media/dhhbew21/scrutiny-findings-report-march-2025.docx)
- Cambridge (June 2026): average cost per general-needs void £2,836, turnaround about 15 days. — [Cambridge ROAM meeting notes, June 2026 (PDF)](https://cambridge.gov.uk/media/a5aowzlg/roam-meeting-notes-2026-06.pdf)
- A Black Country council: average cost per void £2,451 in May 2025, down from £5,695 in 2024/25. Standard repair time fell from 26 to 10 days (council not named in snippet). — [Black Country Radio](https://www.blackcountryradio.co.uk/news/local-headlines/council-cuts-spending-on-empty-home-repairs/)
- Targets: Runnymede 15 working days maximum ([PDF](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)); Southampton 10 days routine and 65 days major ([report](https://southampton.gov.uk/modernGov/documents/s76717/Housing%20Voids%20Governance%20Committee%20Report%20and%20Plan.pdf)); Havering average 53.7 days against a London top quartile of 66.3 ([Havering report](https://democracy.havering.gov.uk/documents/s83231/Places%20OSSC%20Report%20-%20Voids.pdf)). Gateshead was criticised for a 95-day turnaround. — [Yahoo/local press](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/95-day-turnaround-empty-gateshead-193621545.html)
- Croydon published a void "deep dive" (Feb 2025); not read. — [Croydon (PDF)](https://www.croydon.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/06.-hib-void-deep-dive-feb-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- A council with 5,000-10,000 homes and normal turnover probably has a few hundred voids a year. This is an estimate, not sourced. A small contractor might handle tens to low hundreds of properties a year per client, each with its own document trail.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced figure for voids per council per year. Council committee reports (Croydon, Havering, Tamworth, South Kesteven) probably hold these numbers but were not read.
- Major void costs (£10k+) are not covered.

## 7. Data protection duties for contractors

### Takeaway
Councils treat repair and void contractors as data processors under UK GDPR. Contracts include a processing schedule with purpose limits, sub-processor approval, security, and return or deletion at the end. Tenant data the contractor gets includes contact, access and vulnerability details. Awaab's Law adds record-keeping that reaches into contractor correspondence.

### Cited Findings
- Council schedules state "The relationship between the parties is Controller & Processor". A processor must not appoint a sub-processor without the controller's written authorisation and is liable for the sub-processor. Use is limited to the stated purpose and the data must not be shared. — [North Northamptonshire schedule, Contracts Finder](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/Attachment/ecb1afb5-a790-4c3c-8fbf-e49993c5ce88)
- At contract expiry, data is returned or deleted "as directed by the Council" (Berneslai Homes/Barnsley). — [Barnsley Appendix E (PDF)](https://barnsleymbc.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s70236/Appendix%20E.pdf)
- Council housing privacy notices name repair contractors as recipients of tenant data (e.g. Leeds, Ipswich maintenance and contracts notice). — [Leeds](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/privacy-and-data/service-privacy-notices/housing-privacy/housing-notice); [Ipswich (PDF)](https://www.ipswich.gov.uk/sites/www.ipswich.gov.uk/files/maintenance_and_contracts_privacy_notice_vjuly_2022.pdf)
- RBKC DPIA: contractors receive contact details, addresses, appointment and access information, and vulnerability indicators. — [RBKC DPIA (PDF)](https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/Committees/documents/s36760/ED5014342R3%20DPIA.pdf)
- Awaab's Law guidance: keep resident and vulnerability information in line with UK GDPR, and record correspondence with contractors. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector)

### Inferences
- An AI assistant a contractor uses on council emails would be a sub-processor (or a tool of the processor). The contractor would probably need the council's written authorisation, UK or adequate-country hosting, and a deletion process at contract end. Vulnerability data counts as sensitive. This argues for UK-region hosting and per-client data separation. This is my inference; it needs legal review.

### Gaps
- I found no voids-specific contract data protection schedule.
- Whether councils allow contractors to put their data into third-party AI tools: no source found.

## 8. Regulatory pressure in late 2026

### Takeaway
Awaab's Law Phase 1 has been in force since 27 Oct 2025, with fixed deadlines for emergency hazards and damp and mould. Phase 2 (2026) adds more hazards, and Phase 3 (2027) covers all HHSRS hazards except overcrowding. A reformed Decent Homes Standard is confirmed, enforceable from 2035. All of this raises the evidence and record-keeping load that flows down to contractors.

### Cited Findings
- The Hazards in Social Housing (Prescribed Requirements) (England) Regulations 2025 came into force on 27 Oct 2025. — [GOV.UK Awaab's Law guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords)
- Timescales:
  - Emergency hazards: investigate within 24 hours and make safe within 24 hours.
  - Significant hazards: investigate within 10 working days.
  - Written summary to the tenant within 3 working days of the investigation ending.
  - Relevant safety work within 5 working days.
  - Supplementary preventative works begin within 5 working days, or physically start within 12 weeks.
  - Alternative accommodation at the landlord's cost if the home cannot be made safe in time.
  — [GOV.UK timeframes guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector)
- Phases:
  - Phase 1: emergency hazards and significant damp and mould.
  - Phase 2 (2026): excess cold and heat, falls, structural collapse and explosions, fire and electrical hazards, hygiene and food safety.
  - Phase 3 (2027): all remaining HHSRS hazards except overcrowding.
  — [GOV.UK timeframes guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector). ### Inferences
- Phase 2 brings electrical and fire hazards into scope. EICR and fire-safety evidence from contractors becomes time-critical, which adds to the case for quickly finding the right certificate for a property.
- A contractor who can produce a dated evidence trail (photos, access attempts, reports) for a property is more valuable to a landlord that has to defend "all reasonable endeavours".
