## 1. Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 (CAR 2012): duty to manage, contractor duties, work categories, training

### Takeaway
The reg 4 "duty to manage" applies to non-domestic premises, which includes the common parts of blocks of flats (stairs, corridors, risers, boiler rooms), but not the inside of individual flats. Inside flats, councils still have duties as employers and as CDM clients, and every contractor has a direct duty under reg 5 not to start work that may disturb asbestos until they have found out (or presumed) whether asbestos is there. The only change to CAR 2012 found for 2025–2026 is about clearance analyst independence (reg 20), laid November 2026; survey quality and NNLW stay guidance-only.

### Cited Findings
**Reg 4, duty to manage (non-domestic premises)**
- Dutyholder is anyone with a contract or tenancy obligation to maintain or repair the premises, or, if there is none, anyone with control of that part. Everyone must cooperate with the dutyholder. — [legislation.gov.uk reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/632/regulation/4)
- The dutyholder must make a "suitable and sufficient assessment" of whether asbestos is or is likely to be present (checking plans, age of the building, inspecting reasonably accessible parts), review it and record conclusions. — [reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/632/regulation/4)
- Where asbestos is or may be present, the dutyholder must prepare a **written plan** that covers monitoring condition, maintenance or safe removal, and **giving information on location and condition to anyone liable to disturb it**, and to the emergency services. The regulation says "written plan", not "register" (the register is HSE guidance terminology). — [reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/632/regulation/4)
- No outstanding amendments to reg 4 listed on legislation.gov.uk (checked Nov 2026). — [reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/632/regulation/4)

**Domestic premises and council flats**
- HSG264 para 10: reg 4 "do[es] not normally apply to domestic premises", but does apply "to common parts of premises, including housing developments and blocks of flats", and places no direct duties on landlords for individual houses or flats. Common parts examples: foyers, corridors, lifts and lift shafts, staircases, boilerhouses, vertical risers, gardens, yards and outhouses. — [HSG264 2nd ed. 2012 (PDF)](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- HSG264 paras 11–12: under HSWA 1974 ss.2–4 and the Management Regs 1999, local authorities and housing associations still have duties to staff and others in domestic premises used as a workplace, and, as employers, must identify asbestos, risk assess and prepare a written plan of work under the general CAR 2012 requirements. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- HSG264 para 13: under CDM 2015, the client must give designers and contractors bidding for the work **project-specific** asbestos information; general references to possible hazards are not acceptable. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)

**Reg 5, what the contractor (employer) must do before work**
- An employer must not carry out demolition, maintenance or other work that exposes or is likely to expose employees to asbestos unless it has (a) made a suitable and sufficient assessment of whether asbestos is present, its type, the material it is in and its condition, or (b) where in doubt, **assumed asbestos is present and that it is not chrysotile alone**, and complied with the rest of the Regulations. — [legislation.gov.uk reg 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/632/regulation/5) (paraphrased by fetch tool; check exact wording)
- HSG264 para 69 (domestic): management arrangements must make sure "asbestos registers or records are checked before work commencing" and there are procedures for suspect material ("stop work, check material etc"), plus tradespeople training and supervision. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- HSG264 para 52 / reg 7: all ACMs must be removed as far as reasonably practicable before major refurbishment or demolition. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)

**Licensed vs notifiable non-licensed (NNLW) vs non-licensed work**
- Licensable examples: sprayed coatings, work that may disturb pipe lagging, loose fill insulation, asbestos millboard; work on asbestos insulating board (AIB) is licensable unless the risk assessment shows it is short duration. — [HSE NNLW page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/licensing/notifiable-non-licensed-work.htm)
- NNLW requires: notification to the enforcing authority **before work starts** (no minimum notice, online form is the only method), designated and signed work areas, medicals (chest exam and lung function, by a licensed doctor, at least every 3 years, certificate kept 4 years), and a register of work and workers kept for 40 years. — [HSE NNLW page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/licensing/notifiable-non-licensed-work.htm)
- NNLW examples: removing substantially damaged/broken asbestos cement (e.g. after fire/flood), large-scale removal of textured coatings by steaming/gelling, minor short-duration repair of a small section of pipe insulation. — [HSE NNLW page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/licensing/notifiable-non-licensed-work.htm)
- Non-licensed (non-notifiable) examples: careful removal of intact asbestos cement roof sheets, painting AIB in good condition, drilling holes in textured coatings to fit fixtures. Still needs controls, information, instruction and training. — [HSE NNLW page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/licensing/notifiable-non-licensed-work.htm)

**Training**
- Three levels: (1) asbestos awareness, for workers whose normal job could disturb building fabric (electricians, plumbers, joiners, painters, plasterers, roofers, demolition workers), to help them avoid disturbing asbestos; (2) non-licensable work including NNLW, for workers who must disturb ACMs (drilling, removing floor tiles, AC roof repair); (3) licensable work. — [HSE training page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/training.htm)
- Awareness: no mandatory full annual refresher, "a realistic, commonsense approach"; non-licensed and licensed: refresher every year or more often if methods change. UKATA and IATP listed as training associations (not endorsed by HSE). — [HSE training page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/training.htm)
- "Category A" (awareness), "Category B" (non-licensable), "Category C" (licensable) are the L143 ACoP labels in common use. **Unverified in this session**: L143 text not opened.

**2025–2026 changes**
- HSE consultation "Legislative and guidance proposals for the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012" ran 10 Nov 2025 to 9 Jan 2026. Outcome page (updated 3 Nov 2026): a Statutory Instrument was **laid in November 2026** amending reg 20 so the clearance analyst must not be appointed by the removal contractor; dutyholders get one year to adapt; L143 and HSG248 to be updated. Surveys: no mandatory accreditation, HSE "continues to recommend" accredited organisations and is developing guidance. NNLW category kept, clearer guidance to come. — [HSE consultation page](https://consultations.hse.gov.uk/hse/proposals-control-of-asbestos-regs-2012/); background [Clyde & Co, Mar 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/03/hse-consultation-legislative-and-guidance-proposal)
- The SI itself was not found on legislation.gov.uk in this session: **unverified** whether made/in force.

### Inferences
- For a small contractor refurbishing a council flat (kitchen, bathroom, rewire), the legally decisive duty is reg 5 on the contractor itself, plus CDM: the council should supply asbestos information, but if it does not, the contractor must not start, or must presume asbestos (not chrysotile only) and work accordingly.
- The AI system's core question per job is: "Is there valid asbestos info covering this exact location and type of work (R&D for intrusive work), and does it show ACMs, presumed ACMs or no-access areas in the work area?"
- Work-category logic (licensed/NNLW/non-licensed) depends on product type plus task plus condition, so the extraction must capture product type and condition, not just "asbestos yes/no".

### Gaps
- L143 ACoP (2nd ed. 2013) not opened; exact training category wording and NNLW decision flowchart not quoted.
- The November 2026 SI text and commencement date not verified.
- legislation.gov.uk OGL statement not re-checked this session.

## 2. HSG264: management survey vs refurbishment and demolition (R&D) survey, and report contents

### Takeaway
HSG264 (2nd ed., 2012, OGL) is still the survey standard in late 2026. A management survey covers normal occupation and routine maintenance; an R&D survey is fully intrusive, limited to the work area, and needed before any refurbishment that disturbs the building fabric (e.g. a council kitchen or bathroom replacement). The guide defines the report sections and the per-item results table that becomes the asbestos register.

### Cited Findings
- HSG264 is the 2nd edition (2012), ISBN 9780717665020, free download; web page updated 7 Aug 2025 but no revision notice; OGL v3.0. — [HSG264 page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/hsg264.htm)
- Survey aims (para 36): record location, extent and product type; accessibility, condition and surface treatment; asbestos type by sampling or presumption. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- **Management survey** (paras 43–50): "the standard survey", to locate ACMs that could be damaged during normal occupancy including foreseeable maintenance and installation, and assess condition; minor intrusion with simple tools; can be all sampled, partly presumed or all presumed; any area not accessed must be presumed to contain asbestos and no work done there until checked. Areas to inspect (Box 4): under floor coverings, ceiling voids, lofts, risers, ducts, lift shafts, basements, cellars. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Para 50: "Refurbishment surveys will be required for all work which disturbs the fabric of the building in areas where the management survey has not been intrusive." — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- **R&D survey** (paras 51–53): needed before any refurbishment or demolition; locates all ACMs in the work area (whole building for demolition); fully intrusive and destructive (lift carpets and tiles, break through walls, ceilings, partitions, open floors); should be done in unoccupied areas; usually no condition assessment unless removal is more than ~3 months away. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Para 57: in R&D surveys, areas not accessed must be clearly located on plans and in the report text. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- **Domestic strategy** (paras 60–69): councils/HAs group stock into archetypes (build date, design, location) and label groups "asbestos free" (generally built 2000 or later, or strong evidence), "contain" or "possibly contain"; management surveys on a proportion of each archetype; every unsurveyed property "has the potential to contain all the ACMs in the range" for its type; R&D surveys "only necessary in the specific area/location where the works will take place, eg cupboard, part of a room, kitchen/bathroom"; urgent work may need the R&D survey just before work. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Para 61: ACM presence varies even within one archetype (offcuts used by builders, unrecorded removal, tenant modifications). — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- **Report sections** (para 132): executive summary; introduction (scope); general site and survey information; survey results (including material assessment); conclusions and actions; bulk analysis results. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Executive summary (para 134): locations with identified or presumed ACMs; areas not accessed (specific, not generic); high-scoring ACMs; actions and priorities. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- General site info (para 136): surveying organisation name and address; surveyor names; commissioning client; premises address; report date; survey date; areas included; areas excluded; method; survey type and where each applies; deviations; agreed exclusions and inaccessible areas with reasons. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Bulk analysis appendix (para 143): lab name and address, method reference, UKAS accreditation number, results table by sample ID (asbestos found/not found and types), analysis dates, analyst and countersignatory names and signatures. Labs must be ISO/IEC 17025 and UKAS accredited if serving third parties (para 31). Photographs should be included (para 144). — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Surveyor competence: HSG264 recommends checking accreditation/certification (UKAS-accredited organisations, or individual qualifications such as BOHS P402, S301/CoCA, RSPH Level 3) plus insurance and track record (Box 3, paras 23–28). — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Client checks on a report (Box 11): check against tender, unagreed caveats/disclaimers, right survey type, clear plans, all rooms accessed, enough samples (usually 1–2 per area/room), discrepancies. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)

### Inferences
- A contractor doing a kitchen/bathroom refit in a council flat should expect (and an AI checker should look for) a **localised R&D survey for that room**, not just a management survey or an archetype result.
- Survey type, date, scope (rooms covered) and no-access list are first-class metadata for retrieval; a "no ACMs found" result is only valid for the rooms and intrusion level surveyed.

### Gaps
- UKAS accreditation standard for inspection bodies (ISO/IEC 17020) not confirmed from a UKAS source this session.

## 3. Layout of a survey report and asbestos register (fields per item)

### Takeaway
HSG264 gives a reference register table (Table 1) and a marked-up floor plan (Figure 5). Per item: location, product type, extent, accessibility, condition, surface treatment, asbestos type, sample number, sampled/presumed/strongly presumed, material assessment score, priority score, action. This is the best public, OGL template for mock documents.

### Cited Findings
- Results table fields (para 137): location (building, floor/level, room ID and position); extent (area, length, thickness, volume); product type; level of identification (presumed, strongly presumed or identified); asbestos type (e.g. chrysotile, amosite, crocidolite). — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Extra fields for management surveys (para 138): accessibility; damage/deterioration; surface treatment; material assessment score or category (high/medium/low/very low); actions. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Results presented room by room; rooms not accessed and presumed to contain asbestos must appear in the table; priority scores listed if done (para 140). Non-asbestos results go in a **separate table** (para 141). — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- HSG264 Table 1 column headings: Location | Product type | Extent | Accessibility | Condition | Surface treatment | Asbestos type | Sample no | Sampled/presumed/strongly presumed | Material assessment score | Priority score | Action. Example row: "Store room 2, BC408 ceiling | AIB | Whole ceiling 120 m2 | Medium | Good | Painted one face only | Amosite | 1 | Sampled 4 samples | 5 | 12". Another: "Plant room 2, BC416, pipe lagging | Pipe insulation | 24 linear metres | Easy | Good | Sealed and labelled | Crocidolite Amosite Chrysotile | 7 | Sampled 6 samples | 8 | 14 | remove during next campaign". Others: cushion floor (paper backing) under vinyl, woven cloth in electrical switch box (strongly presumed), lift motor brake shoes ("'H' Vac dust"), AIB wall panels ("monitor weekly"). — [HSG264 PDF, p.43](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Figure 5: building plan with room codes (BC401…BC446) and a legend with two shadings: "Contains asbestos" and "Not accessed, presumed to contain asbestos". — [HSG264 PDF, p.44](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Two presumption levels (para 37): **strong presumption** (looks like a known ACM, similar material confirmed by lab, product commonly made with asbestos); **default presumption** (insufficient evidence, or areas not accessed). Para 126: presumed or strongly presumed ACMs are scored as crocidolite (score 3) unless strong evidence shows otherwise. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Para 38: materials can be presumed asbestos-free only with strong evidence; date cut-offs: post-1985 for amphibole products such as AIB, post-1990 for textured coatings (banned 1988), post-1999 for chrysotile products. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- **Material assessment algorithm** (paras 125–127, Appendix 4): four factors, summed to 2–12:
  - Product type: 1 = composites (vinyl tiles, asbestos cement, textured/decorative coatings, mastics, felts); 2 = AIB, millboard, textiles, gaskets, ropes, paper, felt; 3 = thermal insulation (pipe/boiler lagging), sprayed, loose asbestos.
  - Damage: 0 good; 1 low (scratches, broken edges); 2 medium; 3 high/delaminated, visible debris.
  - Surface treatment: 0 composites; 1 enclosed sprays/lagging, AIB with painted/encapsulated face, AC sheets; 2 unsealed AIB, encapsulated lagging/sprays; 3 unsealed lagging/sprays.
  - Asbestos type: 1 chrysotile; 2 amphibole excluding crocidolite (e.g. amosite); 3 crocidolite.
  - Bands: 10+ high; 7–9 medium; 5–6 low; 4 or less very low. Non-asbestos not scored. — [HSG264 PDF, p.67](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Priority assessment (para 128) factors: location, extent, use of location, occupancy, activities, likelihood/frequency of maintenance; done by the dutyholder, not the surveyor alone (para 129). Remedial options: protect/enclose, seal/encapsulate, repair, else remove (para 130). The priority scoring scheme itself is in HSG227 (not opened). — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Register vs report (paras 146–149): the register is simpler than the report (no bulk analysis or site info), a "living document" updated on removal, new sampling or condition change; paper or database; "Printed versions of an electronic document will usually be needed for contractors"; must be consulted for all work that may disturb fabric "includ[ing] simple and short duration work such as single hole-drilling"; info should reach contractors at tender/engagement, since giving it "when they arrive on site" is unlikely to allow time for controls; the contractor should acknowledge the ACMs. — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)
- Vendor software (PSI2000 manual) implements material + priority scoring in registers — shows commercial registers follow the HSG264 model. — [manula.com PSI2000 glossary](https://www.manula.com/manuals/psi2000/glossary-of-terms/1/en/topic/the-risk-assessment-algorithm) (vendor, not OGL)

### Inferences
- Mock document schema (proposed, not a standard): `property_uprn/address, block, flat no, survey_ref, survey_type (management | R&D | re-inspection), survey_date, surveyor_org, UKAS no, scope/rooms covered, no_access[] (location, reason), items[] {item_id, location (floor, room, position), product_type, extent+unit, accessibility, condition/damage score, surface treatment score, asbestos_type, sample_no, identification (sampled|strongly presumed|presumed), material_score, priority_score, risk_band, recommendation/action, photo_ref}, negative_results[], lab certificate {lab, UKAS no, method, sample results}, caveats`.
- Typical flat items to mock (based on HSG264 product categories and the date cut-offs): textured coating (Artex) ceilings, vinyl floor tiles and bitumen adhesive, AIB behind boiler/airing cupboard or fire door panels, asbestos cement panels/flue/soffits, toilet cisterns, bath panels. These are **inferences** for realism; confirm with a real council register before relying on them.
- Extraction risk: scanned PDFs with wide tables split over lines (as seen when extracting HSG264 Table 1); a parser must handle multi-line cells.

- No real, publicly downloadable council **per-property** survey report or register was found this session (councils publish policies, not registers, likely for privacy/security). Real sample reports from UKAS surveyors were not opened.
- HSG227 priority assessment table not extracted.

## 4. How councils and social landlords share asbestos info with contractors; regulator guidance

### Takeaway
Councils hold the register in their housing/asset management IT system and expect contractors to check it before work and to stop if they find suspect material. Croydon (policy reviewed March 2025) is a clear, citable example. The Regulator of Social Housing's Safety and Quality Standard (from 1 April 2024) is the regulatory frame, but no RSH asbestos-specific guidance was found.

### Cited Findings
- Croydon: register held in the housing management system "NEC", to be made "freely accessible to those likely to disturb the fabric of the building"; staff must check the register before commissioning works and make sure the contractor's RAMS reflects it; asbestos surveyor instructs R&D surveys "for sufficient pre-construction information"; surveyors must keep UKAS accreditation. — [Croydon Asbestos Management Policy (July 2025 PDF, reviewed March 2025)](https://www.croydon.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/asbestos-management-policy-july-2025.pdf)
- Croydon contractor duties (8.10): awareness training for all staff; consult asbestos registers before work; use a licensed subcontractor where work involves ACMs (unless licensed); RAMS and statutory notices in place; if suspect material found, "immediately suspend operations"; send test certificates, certificates of reoccupation and waste evidence within 10 working days; if a pre-2000 property has not been surveyed "the job must be postponed until a survey is carried out". — [Croydon policy PDF](https://www.croydon.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/asbestos-management-policy-july-2025.pdf)
- Portsmouth: staff and contractors review the register before repairs; register on a shared IT system (from search summary, PDF not opened: **unverified detail**). — [Portsmouth asbestos policy Oct 2024](https://www.portsmouth.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/asbestos-policy-oct-2024-aa-accessible.pdf)
- Tendring: register for communal areas of blocks of flats (search summary, not opened). — [Tendring Asbestos Management Policy 2025](https://tdcdemocracy.tendringdc.gov.uk/documents/s73633/A5%20Appendix%20B%20-%20Asbestos%20Management%20Policy%202025.pdf)
- Medway: contractors working on ACMs submit their plan for scrutiny; register updated after damage, maintenance or works (search summary, not opened). — [Medway asbestos management plan (docx)](https://www.medway.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/4593/asbestos_management_plan.docx)
- Other recent council housing asbestos policies (not opened): [Havering 2025](https://www.havering.gov.uk/downloads/file/5400/housing-asbestos-control-policy-2025), [Barking & Dagenham 2026](https://www.lbbd.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/Property%20and%20Assets%20-%20Asbestos%20Policy%20Final%20Version.pdf), [Haringey](https://www.minutes.haringey.gov.uk/documents/s150945/Appendix%201%20-%20Asbestos%20Safety%20Policy.pdf), [St Albans housing plan 2019](https://www.stalbans.gov.uk/sites/default/files/documents/publications/housing/housing-publications/SADC%20Housing-Management%20of%20Asbestos%20Plan.doc%20Final%2013%20June%202019_tcm15-67924.pdf).
- RSH consumer standards (Safety and Quality Standard among four) apply from 1 April 2024 under the Social Housing (Regulation) Act 2023, with proactive inspections of landlords with 1,000+ homes. — [Central Bedfordshire summary](https://www.centralbedfordshire.gov.uk/housing/social-housing-regulatory-reforms/consumer-standards); [RSH Consumer Regulation Review 2023-24](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/consumer-regulation-review-2023-to-2024)
- HSE: register info should go to contractors at tender/engagement, printed copies usually needed for contractors (HSG264 paras 148–149). — [HSG264 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)

### Inferences
- Realistic flow for a small contractor: work order from council repairs/planned works system with an attached or linked asbestos record (register extract or survey PDF) per property/block; for kitchens/bathrooms the council (or its surveying contractor) commissions a localised R&D survey before work. Exact attachment format (PDF per property vs portal) varies by council: **inference, not verified**.
- Two separate scopes per flat: the communal block register (reg 4) and the in-dwelling survey/archetype data. The AI system should search both.

### Gaps
- No RSH document specifically about asbestos found; the exact Safety and Quality Standard wording on health and safety not retrieved.
- No public documentation of contractor portals (e.g. which housing systems expose asbestos data to contractors) found.

## 5. Typical failures and enforcement cases

### Takeaway
Typical failures: no R&D survey before refurbishment, relying on a management survey, client not passing survey results to the contractor, untrained workers, continuing after suspect material is found. Most well-reported cases are older (2010–2021); no 2025–2026 council-flat case was found.

### Cited Findings
- ASAI Construction (principal contractor), flat refurbishment in St John's Wood: no R&D survey; kept working after a subcontractor reported asbestos; fined £30,000 (2021 report; from search summary). — [HSM: £30k fine](https://www.hsmsearch.com/workers-exposed-asbestos-30k-fine)
- Sutton Grove housing estate, Sutton (July 2016): Wildheart instructed Squaredeal to replace soffits on three blocks of flats without checking for asbestos (from search summary; client type not stated). — [construction.co.uk](https://www.construction.co.uk/construction-news/245163/company-and-contractor-prosecuted-for-asbestos-safety-failings)
- Rotherham council's ALMO allowed a plumber to be exposed to up to 50 times the legal limit (2011). — [Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2011/01/12/almo-exposes-plumber-to-50x-asbestos-limit/)
- North Warwickshire Borough Council fined £5,000 for sending a decorating contractor to remove asbestos board at a community centre without passing on survey results (2013). — [Irwin Mitchell](https://www.irwinmitchell.com/newsandmedia/2013/november/council-and-decorating-firm-prosecuted-for-asbestos-failings-918009)
- Nuneaton and Bedworth BC fined £5,000 (2010) after an employee fixed a leak in a housing development without being told asbestos was present (search summary). — [Local Government Lawyer](https://www.localgovernmentlawyer.co.uk/housing-law/315-housing-features/4189-local-authority-fined-for-breach-of-asbestos-regulations)
- Newnham College flat refurbishment, Cambridge (offence 2018, reported 2020): no refurbishment survey, asbestos insulation debris in floor voids, an employee without awareness training; breach of CAR regs 5 and 16; £12,000 plus costs. — [Housing Compliance](https://www.housingcompliance.co.uk/asbestos-hse-prosecutions-for-failings/)
- Mansell and Woodlands Plant Hire, flat refurbishment, £50,000 (2011). — [Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2011/03/28/mansell-hit-with-50000-asbestos-fine/)
- Thistlemoor Healthcare and Management, £15,000, breach of reg 5 on two refurbishment projects (2018, search summary). — [construction.co.uk](https://www.construction.co.uk/construction-news/248075/property-management-company-fined-for-asbestos-safety-failings)
- City Building (Glasgow), £4,000: failed to realise a kitchen cupboard door had not been surveyed (Scotland, not England; search summary). — [SHP](https://www.shponline.co.uk/in-court/asbestos-exposure-risk-overlooked-by-lack-of-survey/amp/)
- HSE press releases tagged asbestos: [press.hse.gov.uk/tag/asbestos](https://press.hse.gov.uk/tag/asbestos) (HSE press releases are OGL; individual releases not opened).

### Inferences
- Good eval scenarios: (a) management survey only, kitchen refit requested; (b) R&D survey covers bathroom, but work extends to hallway; (c) "no access" loft/riser in work area; (d) pre-2000 flat with no survey; (e) presumed item in a survey older than the last refurbishment.

### Gaps
- Most case details come from search summaries and secondary news, not HSE press release text. Fine amounts and dates should be checked on press.hse.gov.uk before use in the presentation.
- No 2025–2026 English social housing case confirmed.

## 6. Romanian translations of HSE asbestos guidance

### Takeaway
HSE has a Romanian page, but no asbestos material in Romanian. The Romanian items cover general worker rights, small construction contractors (INDG344), cement, welfare and back pain.

### Cited Findings
- HSE Romanian page lists: INDG450 "Securitatea dumneavoastră, sănătatea dumneavoastră", INDG410 for workers from abroad, the law leaflet, "Atenţie la paşi în industria construcţiilor", INDG344 (health and safety essentials for small construction contractors), CIS57, CIS26 (cement), CIS59 (welfare), INDG404. None on asbestos (azbest). — [HSE Romanian languages page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/languages/romanian.htm)
- EU-OSHA has a Romanian-URL page on asbestos risk guidelines; unclear whether content is Romanian. — [EU-OSHA /ro/](https://osha.europa.eu/ro/themes/dangerous-substances/practical-tools-dangerous-substances/guidelines-managing-asbestos-related-health-and-safety-risks-work)

### Inferences
- A Romanian-language explainer of asbestos checks would fill a real gap for Romanian tradespeople in England; any translation of OGL HSE text must keep the attribution and must not imply HSE endorsement.
- INDG344 (Romanian) may include a short asbestos section, since it covers small construction contractors: **unverified**, not opened.

### Gaps
- INDG344 Romanian PDF contents not checked for asbestos.
- No Romanian government (ITM/INSEMEX) asbestos guidance checked.
