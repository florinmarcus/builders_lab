# UK rules on AI (as of 18 Nov 2026) and what councils require from suppliers who use AI

Scope: England. builders_lab = AI assistant for small Romanian-run contractors refurbishing council flats; reads council emails and attachments (may include tenant personal data), organises them per property, answers in Romanian (Claude on Amazon Bedrock).

Not legal advice. "Interpretation" marks my reading, not a source's. Many 2026 facts come from law-firm or commentary pages (dated in the line); primary sources (gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, ico.org.uk) should be checked before the presentation relies on them. Items marked "(training knowledge, verify)" are from my background knowledge with the official URL given, not re-read in this session.

## 1. UK approach: white paper, regulators, AI bill, Action Plan, copyright

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026 the UK still has no AI Act. AI is governed by existing law (data protection, consumer, equality, product, sector rules) applied by existing regulators, following the 2023 white paper. No AI bill was in the King's Speech of 13 May 2026; the frontier-AI bill promised since 2024 has not been introduced. The copyright reform was shelved in March 2026: current copyright law stands.

### Cited Findings
- The March 2023 white paper "A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation" set five cross-sector principles (safety/security/robustness; transparency and explainability; fairness; accountability and governance; contestability and redress) to be applied by existing regulators, not a new law (training knowledge, verify) — [GOV.UK white paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-regulation-a-pro-innovation-approach/white-paper)
- In Feb 2025 the government said most AI systems should be regulated at the point of use by existing expert regulators; the government committed to "appropriate legislation" for the most powerful models, but that bill has not been introduced (article 18 Jun 2026) — [Scaffold Digital](https://www.scaffold.digital/news/uk-ai-regulation-in-2026-whats-in-force-whats-coming-and-what-your-business-should-do)
- No UK AI Act; no AI Bill before Parliament as of May 2026; 2026 legislative slot "looks tight"; expects regulator rule-making rather than primary legislation over the next 12–18 months (published 10 Apr 2026, updated 25 Oct 2026) — [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/uk-ai-regulation-what-the-law-says/)
- King's Speech of 13 May 2026 announced no AI bill; Lord Holmes's private member's Artificial Intelligence (Regulation) Bill fell at end of session on 30 Apr 2026 (search-result summary of commentary, not checked on parliament.uk) — [io-sphere guide](https://io-sphere.io/guides/ai-regulation-uk-2026); [vorplabs](https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/united-kingdom)

- Interpretation: the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill targets medium/large managed service providers; a tiny SaaS start-up is unlikely to be in scope at launch. Watch it.

## 2. Data protection: UK GDPR, DPA 2018 as amended by DUAA 2025, ICO guidance

### Takeaway
This is the law that binds us most. DUAA 2025 (Royal Assent 19 Jun 2025) made its main data protection changes live on 5 Feb 2026, including new Articles 22A–22D (automated decisions allowed with safeguards, except for special category data) and "recognised legitimate interests". The ICO must now write a statutory AI and ADM code (duty from 12 May 2026; code not yet published, expected 2027). For builders_lab the core points are controller/processor roles, a DPIA, Article 28 processor terms, and sub-processor flow-down to AWS/Anthropic.

### Cited Findings
- Main DUAA data protection provisions commenced 5 Feb 2026 via SI 2026/82 (Commencement No. 6); complaints-by-data-subjects provisions commenced 19 Jun 2026 — [LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/key-provisions-of-data-use-access-act-2025-to-come-into-force-on-5-february-2026); [Bratby Law, commencement](https://bratby.law/data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement/)
- Section 80 DUAA replaced UK GDPR Art 22 with Arts 22A–22D from 5 Feb 2026; Schedule 4 added Art 6(1)(ea) "recognised legitimate interests" — [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/uk-ai-regulation-what-the-law-says/); [Licentium](https://www.licentium.io/post/uk-automated-decision-making-rules-under-data-use-and-access-act-2025-enter-force-february-2026)
- Old Art 22 was a default prohibition on solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects; new rules permit them with safeguards (information, human intervention, right to contest) — default moved from "prohibited unless permitted" to "permitted if safeguards exist" — [Scaffold Digital](https://www.scaffold.digital/news/uk-ai-regulation-in-2026-whats-in-force-whats-coming-and-what-your-business-should-do); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/uks-data-protection-reforms-take-effect-a-new-era-for-automated-decision-making/)
- Restriction remains for significant decisions based on special category data (needs explicit consent or substantial public interest conditions) (training knowledge, verify) — [legislation.gov.uk DUAA 2025 s80](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/80)
- DUAA text — [legislation.gov.uk DUAA 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents)
- SI 2026/425 (DPA 2018 (Code of Practice on AI and Automated Decision-Making) Regulations 2026) in force 12 May 2026: Commissioner must prepare a single statutory code on AI development, deployment and ADM, with a children's data component; code not drafted/published; Bratby expects 2027 — [Bratby Law, SI 2026/425](https://bratby.law/ico-ai-biometrics-code-of-practice/); [Scaffold Digital](https://www.scaffold.digital/news/uk-ai-regulation-in-2026-whats-in-force-whats-coming-and-what-your-business-should-do); [Info-Gov](https://info-gov.uk/artificial-intelligence/artificial-intelligence-news/government-lays-regulations-requiring-ico-to-produce-statutory-ai-and-automated-decision-making-code-of-practice)
- ICO is being replaced by the Information Commission (DUAA s119); one source says the duty moved to the Information Commission on 30 Sep 2026 — [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/uk-ai-regulation-what-the-law-says/). Only one source found for this date: unconfirmed.
- ICO consultation on draft ADM and profiling guidance opened 31 Mar 2026, closed 29 May 2026; final guidance expected summer 2026 — [Scaffold Digital](https://www.scaffold.digital/news/uk-ai-regulation-in-2026-whats-in-force-whats-coming-and-what-your-business-should-do); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102mql9/icos-ai-code-of-practice-on-the-horizon/)
- ICO to publish guidance helping councils and other public bodies procure AI tools (listing 2 Jun 2026; publication not confirmed) — [Local Government Lawyer](https://www.localgovernmentlawyer.co.uk/procurement-and-contracts/402-procurement-news/100662-ico-sets-out-plans-for-statutory-ai-code-of-practice-and-procurement-guidance2)
- ICO generative AI consultation outcomes report (12 Dec 2024): legitimate interests is the only available basis for training on web-scraped personal data; necessity and balancing tests are hard to meet; controllership across the AI supply chain must be assessed on facts — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/01/ico-publishes-outcomes-of-genai-consultation); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/ico-publish-outcome-report-on-ai-consultation-series)
- ICO "Guidance on AI and data protection" (accountability, DPIA, lawfulness, fairness, transparency, accuracy, individual rights) remains the core ICO AI guidance (training knowledge, verify; may be revised after DUAA) — [ICO guidance on AI and data protection](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/)
- DPIA: ICO lists "innovative technology" including AI as a factor making processing likely high risk, so a DPIA is expected (training knowledge, verify) — [ICO DPIA guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/)
- Processor rules: UK GDPR Art 28 requires a written contract with set terms; a processor may not engage a sub-processor without the controller's prior written authorisation and must flow down the same obligations; Art 28(4) keeps the processor liable for its sub-processors (training knowledge, verify) — [legislation.gov.uk UK GDPR Art 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/28); [ICO controllers and processors](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/controllers-and-processors/)
- UK GDPR maximum fines £17.5m or 4% of worldwide turnover; DUAA raised PECR fines to the same level (training knowledge, verify) — [ICO penalties](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/law-enforcement/guide-to-le-processing/penalties/)

### Inferences
- Interpretation, roles: the council is controller of tenant data. The contractor receives it as the council's processor (or as an independent controller for its own business purposes, depending on the council contract). builders_lab is then the contractor's processor, i.e. a sub-processor to the council chain, and AWS (Bedrock) is our sub-processor. Under Art 28(2)/(4), the contractor may need the council's written consent before passing council data to us. This is the single biggest legal/commercial risk: many council contracts forbid onward transfer or offshore processing without consent.
- Interpretation: keep processing in a UK AWS region (eu-west-2 London) if Bedrock offers the Claude model there, or document the transfer (UK IDTA/addendum). Bedrock states customer prompts are not used for model training (verify in AWS terms for the presentation).
- Interpretation: builders_lab organises and answers; it does not make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on tenants, so Arts 22A–22D are unlikely to bite, provided we do not, e.g., auto-decide tenant access refusals or charges. Keep a human in the loop and say so.
- Interpretation: recognised legitimate interests (Art 6(1)(ea)) covers listed purposes (e.g. crime, safeguarding, emergencies), not commercial product processing; our lawful basis comes from the council chain (processor acting on instructions).
- Interpretation: a DPIA is expected before go-live (new technology + tenant data, possibly vulnerable tenants, special category data in repairs emails e.g. health/disability adaptations). We should give contractors a ready DPIA template and Art 28 terms.

## 3. Public sector: what councils must do, and what they ask of suppliers using AI

### Takeaway
ATRS is mandatory for central government departments and some arm's-length bodies, only recommended for councils. The GDS AI Playbook (Feb 2025) is guidance aimed at government, used by councils as good practice. Procurement guidance (PPN 02/24, replaced by PPN 017 under the Procurement Act 2023 from 24 Feb 2025) asks bidders to disclose AI use, information-only. In practice councils are auditing suppliers for AI use and about 30% already have clauses requiring suppliers to declare AI use. The real obligations reach a subcontractor through the council's contract and data processing terms, plus the council's own Public Sector Equality Duty and data protection duties.

### Cited Findings
- ATRS is mandatory for all government departments and for ALBs that deliver public or frontline services or deal directly with the public (scope and exemptions policy, Dec 2024) — [GOV.UK ATRS hub](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-hub); [GDS data blog, 8 May 2025](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/2025/05/08/making-the-algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-atrs-mandatory-across-government/); restated Nov 2026 — [Resultsense, 12 Nov 2026](https://www.resultsense.com/insights/2026-11-12-rewiring-the-state-ai-civil-service/)
- Government "recommends" ATRS to other public bodies "such as local government, even if they're not currently mandated" — [GOV.UK Data and AI Ethics Framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework)
- Commentary expects extension to all public bodies, no confirmed date — [Handley Gill](https://www.handleygill.co.uk/handley-gill-blog/public-sector-algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard). Wales recommends ATRS with no mandate — [Digital Public Services Wales](https://digitalpublicservices.gov.wales/guidance-and-standards/recommended-standards/standards-catalogue/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard)
- Cabinet Secretary Antonia Romeo LSE lecture "Rewiring the state" 10 Nov 2026: no mention of suppliers or procurement; little on local government — [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/insights/2026-11-12-rewiring-the-state-ai-civil-service/)
- AI Playbook for the UK Government, GDS, published 10 Feb 2025: 10 principles for civil servants and public sector bodies; covers how to select, buy and deploy AI; builds on the white paper principles; replaces the Generative AI Framework for HMG — [GOV.UK AI Playbook](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-playbook-for-the-uk-government); [GDS blog](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2025/02/10/launching-the-artificial-intelligence-playbook-for-the-uk-government/). ### Inferences
- Interpretation: our contractors are usually subcontractors or framework contractors on repairs/voids/refurbishment contracts, not AI procurements. PPN 017 disclosure is about AI in bids; but a council auditing suppliers may ask "do you use AI on our data?". Contractors should be able to answer yes with a one-page AI-use statement (what tool, where data is held, who sees it, human review, no training on data).
- Interpretation: ATRS does not apply to a contractor's internal tool. If a council used builders_lab itself on tenant-facing decisions, the council might choose to publish an ATRS record; that is unlikely for our use case.
- Interpretation: the binding requirements come from the council contract and data processing agreement (confidentiality, no sub-processors without consent, UK data location, security standards such as Cyber Essentials, breach notification times, deletion at contract end, audit rights). We should design for these: UK region, deletion per property, audit log, Cyber Essentials for builders_lab itself.

## 4. Consumer and liability

### Takeaway
Our customers are businesses, so the Consumer Rights Act 2015 mostly does not apply (it protects consumers). Business-to-business terms fall under contract law and the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (reasonableness test for liability caps). Negligence and negligent misstatement may apply to wrong answers if relied on. The Law Commission is consulting (8 Oct 2026 to 14 Jan 2027) on bringing software and AI within product liability (Consumer Protection Act 1987); proposal only.

### Cited Findings
- Law Commission consultation opened 8 Oct 2026, closes 14 Jan 2027: proposes widening "product" to include software and AI systems (digital or embedded) and some platform design features, within the Consumer Protection Act 1987; proposes a rebuttable presumption of defect for cases with "exceptional evidential difficulties" including AI; final recommendations expected late 2027 — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/law-commission-consultation-signals-possible-major-changes-to-the-product-liability-regime-in-england-and-wales/); [SCL](https://www.scl.org/law-commission-consults-on-modernising-product-liability-for-software-ai-and-digital-platforms/); [Legal IT Insider](https://legaltechnology.com/law-commission-proposes-sweeping-product-liability-reforms-to-bring-ai-and-software-within-scope/)
- EU Product Liability Directive must be implemented by member states by 9 Dec 2026 (relevant only for EU sales) — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/consumer-protection/your-software-is-about-to-become-a-product-uk-reform-proposals-and-the-eus-december-deadline)
- CPA 1987 product liability covers death, personal injury and damage to private property, not pure economic loss (training knowledge, verify) — [legislation.gov.uk CPA 1987 s5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/43/section/5)
- Consumer Rights Act 2015 Part 1 Chapter 3 gives consumers rights for digital content (satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described) and for services (reasonable care and skill, s49); applies to traders supplying consumers only (training knowledge, verify) — [legislation.gov.uk CRA 2015 s34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/34); [s49](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/49)
- B2B: Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982 s13 implies reasonable care and skill; Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 s2 bans excluding liability for death/personal injury from negligence and subjects other exclusions to reasonableness (training knowledge, verify) — [UCTA 1977 s2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1977/50/section/2); [SGSA 1982 s13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1982/29/section/13)
- Equality Act 2010: service providers must not discriminate and must make reasonable adjustments (s29, s20) (training knowledge, verify) — [legislation.gov.uk s29](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/29)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: main liability risk is a wrong answer (e.g. wrong deadline, wrong scope of works, missed tenant vulnerability flag) causing financial loss or a safety issue (asbestos, gas, fire doors). Mitigate with citations to the source email/attachment in every answer, "check the original" warnings, refusing safety-critical advice, and reasonable B2B terms with a liability cap.
- Interpretation: Romanian-language answers increase the risk of mistranslation of contract terms; show the English source text next to the Romanian answer.
- Interpretation, equality: a Romanian-language tool improves access for Romanian-speaking workers. Risk: tenant data on disability/vulnerability appears in emails; the tool must surface it accurately, not drop it, since the council's PSED and the contractor's reasonable-adjustment duties to tenants depend on it.
- Interpretation: if the Law Commission proposals become law (not before late 2027 recommendations, then legislation), AI software could carry strict liability for personal injury/property damage. Low near-term impact.

### Gaps
- No UK case law on liability for wrong AI output to a business found this session.
- Law Commission's own consultation paper not read directly; 25-year longstop figure in one summary unconfirmed.

## 5. Comparison: UK vs EU AI Act (Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
The EU has a horizontal AI law with risk classes and penalties; the UK has none and relies on existing laws and regulators. The EU Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) delayed high-risk duties to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I), but transparency duties (Art 50) apply from 2 Aug 2026. builders_lab serves England, so the EU AI Act only matters if we later serve businesses in Romania or our outputs are used in the EU.

### Cited Findings
- Digital Omnibus: provisional agreement 7 May 2026 (one source says 6 May); published OJ 24 Jul 2026; in force 27 Jul 2026; Annex III high-risk moved 2 Aug 2026 → 2 Dec 2027; Annex I → 2 Aug 2028; transparency regime not deferred; watermark grace for systems already on market to 2 Dec 2026; new prohibitions (non-consensual intimate imagery, CSAM) from 2 Dec 2026 — [regulation-ai.eu](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/omnibus/); [Winston Taylor](https://www.winstontaylor.com/insights/ai-act-rules-on-high-risk-ai-delayed-as-ai-digital-omnibus-agreed); [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/digital-omnibus/); [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/uk-ai-regulation-what-the-law-says/)
- EU GPAI rules applied from Aug 2025; Art 50 transparency from 2 Aug 2026 — [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/uk-ai-regulation-what-the-law-says/)
- EU maximum fines €35m or 7% of worldwide turnover (prohibited practices) — [Scaffold Digital](https://www.scaffold.digital/news/uk-ai-regulation-in-2026-whats-in-force-whats-coming-and-what-your-business-should-do); other tiers €15m/3% and €7.5m/1% (training knowledge, verify) — [AI Act Art 99](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/)
- Art 50(1): providers must ensure people are told they are interacting with an AI system unless obvious (training knowledge, verify) — [AI Act Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- AI Act territorial scope covers providers placing systems on the EU market and providers/deployers outside the EU where output is used in the EU (training knowledge, verify) — [AI Act Art 2](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/)
