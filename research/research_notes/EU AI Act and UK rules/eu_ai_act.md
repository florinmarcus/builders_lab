# EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689): status as of November 2026 and what it means for builders_lab

Notes checked 18 Nov 2026. Not legal advice. "Interpretation" or "Inference" marks my reading, not a source's. "Proposed" and "adopted" are kept apart. Main amending act: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 ("Digital Omnibus on AI"), adopted 8 Jul 2026, published 24 Jul 2026, in force 27 Jul 2026.

## 1. Timeline: what applied on schedule, and what changed

### Takeaway
The early dates (entry into force 1 Aug 2024, prohibitions and AI literacy 2 Feb 2025, GPAI and governance/penalties 2 Aug 2025) kept to schedule. The Digital Omnibus on AI was **adopted** (not just proposed). It moved Annex III high-risk duties to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I high-risk duties to 2 Aug 2028. Article 50 transparency still applied from 2 Aug 2026, with only a short grace period (to 2 Dec 2026) for watermarking by systems already on the market.

### Cited Findings
- The Omnibus is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026, amending Regulations (EU) 2024/1689, 2018/1139 and 2023/1230, published OJ L 24.7.2026 — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)
- It enters into force "on the third day following that of its publication", i.e. 27 July 2026 — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/global/intellectual-property/art/digital-omnibus-ai-has-been-published); [Kinstellar, Jul 2026](https://www.kinstellar.com/news-and-insights/detail/4619/the-ai-act-after-the-digital-omnibus-simplified-rules-delayed-deadlines-but-can-compliance-wait)
- Path to adoption: provisional political agreement in trilogue on 6/7 May 2026, confirmed by Member State representatives 13 May 2026 (Gibson Dunn, 27 May 2026, then still "expected" formal adoption before 2 Aug 2026) — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); Council final approval reported as 29 Jun 2026 — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/) (secondary source; EUR-Lex gives the signing date 8 Jul 2026)
- Amended Art 113: Chapter III Sections 1–3 (high-risk) apply from "2 December 2027 as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article 6(2) and Annex III" and from 2 August 2028 for Annex I (Art 6(1)) systems — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744, point 40(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)
- New Art 111(4): providers of generative AI systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026 "shall take the necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026" — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744, point 39(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)
- Rest of Art 50 (incl. telling users they talk to AI) applied from 2 Aug 2026 unchanged — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Faegre Drinker, Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)
- New Art 5 prohibition (AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM) applies from 2 Dec 2026 — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744, point 40(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- GPAI model obligations still apply from 2 Aug 2025, no date change — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Enforcement/penalty framework applies from 2 Aug 2026 (fines up to EUR 15m or 3% for most infringements) — [Kinstellar](https://www.kinstellar.com/news-and-insights/detail/4619/the-ai-act-after-the-digital-omnibus-simplified-rules-delayed-deadlines-but-can-compliance-wait)
- Member States must have at least one AI regulatory sandbox "operational by 2 August 2027" (one year later than the original 2 Aug 2026) — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744, point 22(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- A consolidated AI Act text dated 27 Jul 2026 exists on EUR-Lex — [EUR-Lex consolidated 2024/1689 (2026-07-27)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng/pdf); Osborne Clarke redline — [Osborne Clarke PDF](https://www.osborneclarke.com/system/files/documents/26/07/31/AI-Act--Redline-Digital-Omnibus-on-AI-%28July-30-2026%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- For builders_lab, the dates that matter are already live: Art 4 (AI literacy, since Feb 2025, now softened) and Art 50 (since 2 Aug 2026). A new product launched now has no grace period for Art 50(2): the Dec 2026 grace period covers only systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026.
- High-risk dates (Dec 2027 / Aug 2028) only matter if a feature ever falls into Annex III (see section 3).

### Gaps
- Did not check whether a second omnibus or further amendment is proposed after Reg. 2026/1744 (one tracker says 2026/1744 is the only amendment so far: [regulation-ai.eu](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/changes/)).
- Secondary sources differ on some dates (Council approval 29 Jun vs signing 8 Jul; one tracker shows "in force 11 Sep 2026"). EUR-Lex dates used above.

## 2. Guidelines, codes of practice and standards

### Takeaway
The Art 50 Code of Practice (marking/labelling AI content) is final and found adequate; final Art 50 guidelines are out (20 Jul 2026). High-risk classification guidelines are still only a draft (19 May 2026), final promised by end of 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Final Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content published 10 Jun 2026 (drafts Dec 2025 and Mar 2026); voluntary; includes EU icons for AI-generated / AI-modified content — [Jones Day, Jun 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/eu-ai-act-unpacked-33-the-final-code-of-practice-on-transparency-of-ai-generate-102n4yx)
- Commission found the Code adequate for Art 50(2), (4) and (5) on 8 Jul 2026; final Art 50 guidelines published 20 Jul 2026 — [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)
- Guidelines content (per Faegre Drinker): AI agents fall under Art 50(1) and must disclose AI nature and on whose behalf they act; "standard editing" exemption covers grammar, spelling, light polishing and now **AI translations**; AI summaries and substantive rewrites still need marking; text marking is simplified where free text cannot carry metadata; a **B2B carve-out** lets providers omit marking where outputs stay inside closed industrial/B2B environments with safeguards (cloud isolation, role-based access), not for public/consumer systems; signatories need watermark-detection interoperability by 2 Feb 2027 — [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)
- Draft high-risk (Art 6) classification guidelines published 19 May 2026 (late: due 2 Feb 2026); three parts (general, Annex I, Annex III); final expected by end 2026; warn that Art 6(3) is not an easy exit, and that excluding high-risk uses in terms of service alone is not enough — [Commission draft guidelines page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/draft-guidelines-high-risk); [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/eu-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-classification-of-high-risk-ai-systems/); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/european-commission-releases-draft-guidelines-on-high-risk-ai-under-the-eu-ai-act)
- The amended Art 50(7) says the Commission "shall encourage and facilitate the drawing up of codes of practice at Union level" on detection, marking and labelling — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744, point 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: the "AI translations need no marking" point is relevant to us (Romanian answers from English sources), but our answers are summaries and new text, not plain translation, so marking probably still applies to them.
- Interpretation: the B2B carve-out may fit builders_lab (closed tool for one firm's own staff, tenant isolation, role-based access). Worth reading the guidelines text itself before relying on it.

### Gaps
- Did not read the primary Art 50 guidelines document or Code (only law-firm summaries). The GPAI Code of Practice (July 2025) and harmonised standards (CEN-CENELEC JTC 21) status were not checked in this pass. The AI system definition guidelines (Feb 2025) not re-checked.

## 3. How builders_lab would likely be classified

### Takeaway
Interpretation: a RAG assistant that reads a contractor's council emails and documents and answers in Romanian with citations is very likely **not high-risk**. It is an AI system that interacts with people and generates text, so Art 50 transparency applies. Plus the Art 4 AI literacy duty.

### Cited Findings
- Annex III high-risk areas: biometrics; critical infrastructure (safety components); education; employment and worker management; access to essential private/public services (e.g. benefits, creditworthiness, life/health insurance pricing, emergency triage); law enforcement; migration and border control; justice and democratic processes — [Annex III text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Annex I lists the EU product-safety laws whose products can make an AI safety component high-risk (machinery, toys, lifts, radio equipment, pressure equipment, medical devices, vehicles, aviation etc.) — [Annex I text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/1/)
- The Omnibus narrowed the "safety component" definition for AI in regulated products — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Art 50(1): providers must design systems that interact directly with people so they are told they are "interacting with an AI system", unless obvious to a reasonably well-informed person — [Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art 50(2): providers of systems generating synthetic text must mark outputs "in a machine-readable format" and detectable, except for "an assistive function for standard editing" or where the system does not "substantially alter the input data" — [Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art 50(4): deployers publishing AI text "to inform the public on matters of public interest" must disclose it, unless human review/editorial control with editorial responsibility; guidelines say routine review is not enough — [Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)
- Art 50(5): information must be clear, distinguishable, and given at the latest at "the first interaction or exposure" — [Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Omnibus moved supervision of AI systems built on a GPAI model **by the same provider** to the AI Office (exclusive competence); others stay with national authorities — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744, point 31(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: none of the Annex III areas fits "summarise my council emails and documents". Risk points to watch: (a) a feature that ranks or monitors workers (employment, Annex III point 4) would be high-risk; (b) a feature that decides on access to essential services or credit would be too; (c) advice that becomes the safety control for structural or fire safety could be argued about, but buildings and construction products are not Annex I products. Interpretation: the Construction Products Regulation is not in Annex I, so the "safety component of a product" route does not apply.
- Roles (interpretation): builders_lab = **provider** of the AI system (we build it and place it on the market under our name). Each building firm = **deployer**. Anthropic = **GPAI model provider** (Claude); AWS = distributor of the model via Bedrock (our reading). Because we and Anthropic are different companies, our system stays with national authorities, not the AI Office.
- Obligations as provider (EU users): build in the "you are talking to an AI" notice (Art 50(1)); machine-readable marking of generated text (Art 50(2)), unless the B2B carve-out or the editing exemption fits; support AI literacy of our own staff (Art 4). As deployer, the firm has Art 4, and Art 50(4) only if it publishes AI text to the public on public-interest matters (unlikely for internal council correspondence).
- Downstream from GPAI: Anthropic must give downstream providers documentation under the GPAI rules (Art 53, in force since 2 Aug 2025). We have no GPAI-model duties unless we fine-tune/modify the model substantially (not planned). Not checked in this pass: exact Art 53 text.

### Gaps
- Final high-risk guidelines not out yet (expected end 2026); recheck classification then.
- No source found that discusses construction-sector RAG assistants specifically.

## 4. AI literacy duty (Art 4)

### Takeaway
Art 4 applies since 2 Feb 2025, to providers and deployers of any AI system, no size threshold. The Omnibus softened it from "ensure a sufficient level" to "take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff".

### Cited Findings
- Amended Art 4: providers and deployers must "take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff"; Commission to publish practical examples; Board adopts recommendations — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)
- Commission had proposed removing the duty entirely; final text revised, not removed; deployers of high-risk systems must still ensure oversight staff are competent — [Kinstellar](https://www.kinstellar.com/news-and-insights/detail/4619/the-ai-act-after-the-digital-omnibus-simplified-rules-delayed-deadlines-but-can-compliance-wait)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: a small Romanian firm using our tool in Romania is a deployer, so Art 4 applies to it, but the bar is now low (some guidance or short training). We can help by shipping a short Romanian "how to use this safely" guide. A UK firm using the tool in the UK is outside scope (section 5).

### Gaps
- Commission practical examples under the new Art 4 not checked.

## 5. Territorial scope: UK-only users vs Romanian users

### Takeaway
Interpretation: (a) UK firms using the tool in the UK, outputs used only in the UK: the AI Act very likely does not apply. (b) Once we serve firms in Romania (or any EU user), we are a third-country provider placing a system on the EU market, so Art 50 and Art 4 apply. No EU authorised representative is required for a non-high-risk system.

### Cited Findings
- Art 2 covers providers placing AI systems on the EU market (wherever established), deployers established or located in the EU, and third-country providers/deployers where "the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union" — [Art 2 (Commission AI Act Service Desk)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2); [artificialintelligenceact.eu Art 2](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/)
- Commentary: Art 2(1)(c) has no intent element (unlike recital 22), so the output trigger reads very broadly — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/a-practical-guide-to-the-extraterritorial-reach-of-the-ai-act/); [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/eu-uk-ai-navigator/article-2)
- Art 22(1): only providers established in third countries making **high-risk** systems available must appoint an EU authorised representative — [Art 22](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/22/)
- UK: no comprehensive AI law as of Sep 2026; dedicated AI Bill has not appeared; next King's Speech "not expected until late 2027 at the earliest" — [Lewis Silkin, 14 Sep 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/14/ai-judgment-day-on-the-horizon-while-uk-lawmakers-play-catch-up); FT (Mar 2026) reported the bill pushed to 2027 over copyright — [Freevacy summary of FT](https://www.freevacy.com/news/financial-times/comprehensive-uk-ai-bill-delayed-again-over-copyright-rules/7184)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: the edge case for UK-only is a UK firm sending AI-written text to an EU recipient (e.g. a Romanian supplier). Read literally, that is "output used in the Union". Cheap fix: build Art 50 disclosure and labelling into the product from day one for all users, so this does not matter.
- Interpretation: serving firms in Romania triggers Art 2(1)(a) (placing on the EU market). Our duties are then the Art 50 provider duties, plus Art 4. Romanian deployer firms have Art 4 and maybe Art 50(4).
- UK rules (UK GDPR, ICO guidance) are a separate topic; not covered here.

### Gaps
- No Commission guidance found on how "output used in the Union" applies to incidental cross-border emails.

## 6. Penalties and enforcement; Romania's authority

### Takeaway
Art 50 breaches fall in the middle fine tier (up to EUR 15m or 3% of worldwide turnover; for SMEs the lower of the two). Romania named ANCOM as market surveillance authority and single point of contact by government memorandum (12 Mar 2026), but the national implementing law was still in preparation as of July 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Art 99(3): prohibited practices up to EUR 35m or 7%; Art 99(4): other obligations up to EUR 15m or 3%, and point (g) covers "transparency obligations for providers and deployers pursuant to Article 50"; Art 99(5): misleading information to authorities up to EUR 7.5m or 1%; Art 99(6): for SMEs and start-ups, "whichever thereof is lower" — [Art 99](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/)
- Omnibus extends SME privileges (simplified technical documentation, proportionate QMS, reduced fines) to **small mid-caps** (<750 staff, turnover ≤ EUR 150m or balance sheet ≤ EUR 129m) — [Kinstellar](https://www.kinstellar.com/news-and-insights/detail/4619/the-ai-act-after-the-digital-omnibus-simplified-rules-delayed-deadlines-but-can-compliance-wait); amended Art 11(1) lets SMEs and SMCs provide Annex IV documentation "in a simplified manner" — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)
- Romania: government memorandum of 12 Mar 2026; ANCOM "acts as the market surveillance authority and single point of contact"; ASF, BNR, ANSPDCP and ADR have sector roles; implementing law still being prepared — [Kinstellar](https://www.kinstellar.com/news-and-insights/detail/4619/the-ai-act-after-the-digital-omnibus-simplified-rules-delayed-deadlines-but-can-compliance-wait); [Chambers Romania 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/romania)
- One commentator says ANCOM can only verify and sanction once the implementing law takes effect, and no public draft had appeared — [The AI Act Guy (Substack)](https://theaiactguy.substack.com/p/who-enforces-the-ai-act-29); others describe ANCOM as "officially designated" — [Mararu](https://www.mararu.com/articles/ancom-ai-market-surveillance-authority-romania) (conflict noted)
- Overview of national plans — [artificialintelligenceact.eu national implementation](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/national-implementation-plans/)

### Inferences
- Inference: in practice, Romanian enforcement against a small limited-risk tool is unlikely in the near term, but the duties apply directly, so build them in anyway.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether a Romanian implementing law (penalties, procedure) was adopted between July and November 2026. Bird & Bird Romania tracker was paywalled (HTTP 402). Check Monitorul Oficial and ANCOM's site.

## 7. SME measures: sandboxes and simpler documentation

### Takeaway
Sandboxes: each Member State must have one running by 2 Aug 2027 (Romania included). SME/SMC simplifications mainly ease high-risk paperwork, which we likely don't need.

### Cited Findings
- Sandbox deadline 2 Aug 2027 — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744, point 22(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)
- Art 17(2) QMS implementation "shall be proportionate to the size of the provider's organisation"; simplified QMS option extended to all SMEs incl. start-ups (recital 28) — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)
- Real-world testing outside sandboxes extended to Annex I high-risk systems — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: for a limited-risk product, the SME measures change little. Lower fine caps (the "lower of" rule) are the main SME benefit for us.

### Gaps
- Whether Romania's sandbox exists yet, and whether a UK-established company can join an EU sandbox, not checked.
