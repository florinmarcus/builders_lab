# How councils contract and pay small refurbishment firms (England)

Checked 7 Nov 2026. Not legal advice. Full report: `reports/Council refurbishment and asbestos.md`. Source notes: `research_notes/Council refurbishment and asbestos/council_contracting.md`. "Unverified" marks items from search snippets or secondary sources.

## Types of work

| Type | What it is | Fit for a small firm |
|---|---|---|
| Voids | Empty homes made ready to re-let | Good. Regional and single-trade lots exist |
| Responsive repairs | Repairs in occupied homes | Large contracts often go to big firms |
| Planned works | Kitchen and bathroom programmes, Decent Homes | Not researched in depth |
| Adaptations | Disabled Facilities Grant works | Good. Some councils run a DPS for this |

- PfH Responsive Repairs, Voids and Associated Works DPS: five lots (multi-trade, general repairs and joinery, plumbing and heating, electrical, specialist). Minimum entry: Gas Safe and NICEIC for lot 1, Gas Safe for lot 3, NICEIC for lot 4. Other requirements set by each landlord. ([PfH DPS brochure](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2630870_DPSResponsiveRepairsVoidsWorks-PfH_082225.pdf))
- PfH says the DPS is worth about £490m and supports small local firms. Snippet only. ([Glass Times](https://glasstimes.co.uk/news/glazing-contractors-needed/))
- East of England EEM DPS includes void works. Snippet only. ([Contracts Finder](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/8fa9376f-3e88-479f-a3ec-f3debc52851b))
- Doncaster runs a DPS for adaptation works. ([Stotles](https://www.stotles.com/explore/notices/59150b15-1257-421d-a2f0-b10d39252de0/dynamic-purchasing-system-for-the-provision-of-housing-adaptation-works))
- Big bundles go to big firms, e.g. Mears, 10 years, £450m at Birmingham (article date unclear). ([Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/?p=510467))
- Gas is often split out. Runnymede: "All gas works are to be carried out by RBC's Specialist contractor". ([Runnymede Void Standard](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25))
- No source measures the share of void work going to small or Romanian-run firms.

## Routes in

1. **DPS.** Join at any time. A landlord can tender "in as little as 10 days" with its own spec and terms. ([PfH DPS brochure](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2630870_DPSResponsiveRepairsVoidsWorks-PfH_082225.pdf); [LHC blog](https://lse.lhcprocure.org.uk/news/blogs/why-small-and-medium-sized-contractors-should-consider-a-dps))
2. **Frameworks** with regional and value-band lots:
   - PfH Responsive Housing Repairs and Void Property Services: 75 firms, £725m over three years, bands up to £1.5m, runs to 31 Jul 2027. ([PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/building-control-news/75-contractors-appointed-to-pfhs-reactive-repairs-framework/164349/); [PfH framework PDF](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2614082_FrameworkResponsiveRepairsVoidsAssociatedWorks_PfH_061125.pdf))
   - Fusion21 £350m repairs and voids framework. Snippet only. ([Fusion21](https://www.fusion21.co.uk/lot/housing-lot-1a))
   - LHC H3 (2026), £1bn, 12 lots, up to three places reserved for micro or small firms on some lots. Not a voids framework. ([Construction Enquirer, 1 May 2026](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/05/01/lhc-launches-1bn-housing-and-demolition-framework/))
   - LHC RM3 refurbishment, regional lots. ([Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/021999-2025)); LHC £360m refurbishment framework for SMEs (title only) ([LHC](https://www.lhcprocure.org.uk/news/news/new-360-million-public-sector-refurbishment-framework-creates-opportunities-for-smes))
3. **Council's own below-threshold quotes** (Contracts Finder or council portal).
4. **Subcontracting** to a main contractor. How Mears, Wates and others recruit subcontractors: no source found.

Procurement Act 2023:

- Existing DPSs must end by 27 Oct 2028 at the latest. ([Bevan Brittan](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2024/can-dynamic-purchasing-systems-be-used-for-below-threshold-contracts/))
- Below-threshold rules in Part 6 (ss. 84–88). No suitability assessment for regulated below-threshold contracts (s.85(1)). ([Bevan Brittan](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2024/can-dynamic-purchasing-systems-be-used-for-below-threshold-contracts/); [Procurement Act 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54))
- Pipeline notice for contracts over £2m (s.93). ([DLA Piper](https://dlapiperintelligence.com/procurement-act))
- **Unverified**: works threshold around £5.4m including VAT. Not checked on GOV.UK.

## Ordering

- Work comes per property as a **works order**, usually priced from a **schedule of rates**.
- M3NHF Schedule of Rates v8 (2023): each item has a 6-character code and a 1-character priority code. Units such as IT (item) and LM (linear metre). Each rate covers "the entire work content" of the repair. No extra payment beyond the schedule, adjusted by a tendered discount. The client's representative decides what is approved. ([M3NHF SoR v8 extract](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/Assets/NoticeBuilder_FileDownload.aspx?id=357559), Welsh notice; same national schedule used in England)
- Peabody's NEC Housing contractor portal: view and note works orders, vary an order and record actual cost, mark complete, apply for payment. Each contractor sees only its own orders. ([Peabody NEC guide](https://www.peabodygroup.org.uk/media/tqylexo0/nec-contractor-portal-v211.pdf))
- Fixflo applies costs "using your schedule of rates". ([Fixflo](https://www.fixflo.com/solutions/social-housing))

## Void process example (Runnymede)

Source for all steps: [Runnymede Void Standard 2022-25](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25).

1. Contractor's responsibility starts "on receipt of a functioning set of keys".
2. Gets pre-termination information from the Contract Administrator.
3. Reports serious defects and rechargeable items "immediately" with photos.
4. Provides the EICR (Code 1 and 2 defects) "before instructions are given". Remedial work after approval.
5. Maximum turnaround 15 working days (a KPI).
6. Joint handover meeting. Keys returned after the council accepts the work.
7. Minor items may be finished within 28 days of occupation.
8. Receipt date entered on "RBC's Contractors' Portal".
9. 12-month defects period.

## Payment

- Payment by portal application or invoice.
- **30 days** from invoice, implied into public contracts (Procurement Act 2023 s.68(2)), cannot be overridden (s.68(6)), not for invalid or disputed invoices (s.68(3)). Carried down to public sub-contracts (s.73). Authorities publish six-monthly payment compliance notices. ([Ward Hadaway](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/procurement-in-a-nutshell/procurement-in-a-nutshell-supply-chain-payment-terms/); [Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/the-evolution-of-sub-contracting-under-the-procurement-act-2023); [GOV.UK payments compliance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-manage-phase/guidance-payments-compliance-notices-html); [legislation.gov.uk Part 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/part/4/crossheading/terms-implied-into-public-contracts))
- **CIS**: 20% registered, 30% unregistered, 0% gross status. Labour only, not materials, VAT or CITB levy. ([GOV.UK CIS](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor/make-deductions-and-pay-subcontractors); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-in/knowledge/publications/ae7499c6/the-uk-construction-industry-scheme))
- **Unverified**: whether councils are CIS "deemed contractors".
- Builder 1 mentioned CIS complications; details not known ([interview](../interviews/builder-1.md)).

## Data protection

- Councils treat repair contractors as **processors** under UK GDPR. "The relationship between the parties is Controller & Processor." No sub-processor without written authorisation. Use limited to the stated purpose. ([North Northamptonshire schedule](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/Attachment/ecb1afb5-a790-4c3c-8fbf-e49993c5ce88))
- At the end, data returned or deleted "as directed by the Council". ([Barnsley Appendix E](https://barnsleymbc.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s70236/Appendix%20E.pdf))
- Privacy notices name repair contractors as recipients. ([Leeds](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/privacy-and-data/service-privacy-notices/housing-privacy/housing-notice); [Ipswich](https://www.ipswich.gov.uk/sites/www.ipswich.gov.uk/files/maintenance_and_contracts_privacy_notice_vjuly_2022.pdf))
- Contractors receive contact, access and vulnerability data. ([RBKC DPIA](https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/Committees/documents/s36760/ED5014342R3%20DPIA.pdf))
- **Our inference, needs legal review**: an AI tool over a contractor's council email is a sub-processor. Likely needs the council's written consent, UK hosting, per-client separation and deletion at contract end. No source says whether councils allow it.

## Awaab's Law and other pressure

- Hazards in Social Housing (Prescribed Requirements) (England) Regulations 2025, in force 27 Oct 2025. ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords))
- Deadlines ([GOV.UK timeframes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector)):
  - Emergency hazards: investigate and make safe within 24 hours.
  - Significant hazards: investigate within 10 working days.
  - Written summary to tenant within 3 working days after investigation.
  - Safety work within 5 working days.
  - Preventative works begin within 5 working days, or start on site within 12 weeks.
- Phases: 1 (Oct 2025) emergency hazards, damp and mould; 2 (2026) cold and heat, falls, structural collapse, fire, electrical, hygiene; 3 (2027) all other HHSRS hazards except overcrowding. **Unverified**: exact Phase 2 date. Shelter says October 2026. ([Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk/professional_resources/news_and_updates/how_awaabs_law_changes_the_rules_on_hazards_in_social_housing))
- Landlords stay responsible when using contractors. Contractors should know the deadlines, "supply information for written summaries promptly" and report hazards. Landlords keep records of access attempts and contractor correspondence, and photo or sample evidence before removing mould. ([GOV.UK timeframes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector))
- Decent Homes Standard (response 28 Jan 2026): enforced from 2035. ([GOV.UK response](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/consultation-on-a-reformed-decent-homes-standard-for-social-and-privately-rented-homes/outcome/consultation-on-a-reformed-decent-homes-standard-for-social-and-privately-rented-homes-government-response); [Inside Housing](https://management.insidehousing.co.uk/home/new-decent-homes-standard-to-apply-to-social-housing-from-2035-95674); [Propertymark](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/resource/decent-homes-standard-2035-criteria-for-compliance-and-exemptions-confirmed.html))
- RSH consumer standards from April 2024: see `domain/asbestos.md`.
