### (d) UK copyright view on ToC and clause titles

Sourced:
- CDPA 1988 s 3(1): "literary work" includes "(a) a table or compilation other than a database" and "(d) a database". https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/3
- s 3A(2): a database is original "if, and only if, by reason of the selection or arrangement of the contents" it is "the author's own intellectual creation". https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/3A
- s 30(1ZA) quotation exception: needs the work to be public, fair dealing, no more than the purpose needs, and acknowledgement. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/30
- Names, titles and short phrases are generally not protected (UK Copyright Service fact sheet P-18, not a court source). https://copyrightservice.co.uk/copyright/p18_copyright_names
- Commercial TDM still needs a licence (s 29A is non-commercial only) - see methodology-sources.md.
- Unverified (snippets; judgment not fetched, bailii returned 403): NLA v Meltwater [2010] EWHC 3099 (Ch) held newspaper headlines can be literary works; Infopaq (CJEU) said an 11-word extract can be protected if it reflects the author's own intellectual creation.

Our inference (not sourced, not legal advice):
- A single clause title ("Scope", "Normative references") is very unlikely to be protected on its own.
- A **whole** ToC (hundreds of chosen headings in a chosen order) could be protected as a table or compilation, and copying all of it into a database is risky. The BSI and JCT ToC PDFs carry "all rights reserved".
- The bigger risk is contract, not copyright: BSI, JCT and Planning Portal terms restrict collecting or storing their site content, and NEC and IET name AI or retrieval systems directly.
- Safe pattern: store only bibliographic facts we write ourselves (designation, title, edition, status, date, publisher, URL, our own one-line summary). Cite clause numbers in answers ("see BS 5250:2021 clause 11") without storing their ToC. Ask BSI (copyright@bsigroup.com) for a quote if ToC data becomes important.
