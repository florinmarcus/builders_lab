# Romanian builders lack a UK compliance assistant

Across roughly 30 AI products and a dozen established software tools for UK construction checked on 7 Nov 2026, **none offers Romanian-language questions and answers over UK building rules, CDM or tax paperwork**, even though Romanians are the single largest foreign group in UK construction (about **5% of the workforce nationally and 19% in London**). The separate pieces builders_lab would need are each already crowded and cheap in English: cited Q&A over the Approved Documents (StructureGPT from free to £69 a month), AI RAMS generators (RAMSReady from £29 one-off or £49 a month), AI quoting apps and AI receptionists (about £39 to £149 a month). So the gap is not any one feature but the combination: Romanian chat, grounded in English UK sources, aimed at small firms and sole traders, and delivered where the community already talks. The source base for that is legally clean at its core, because legislation, the Approved Documents, HSE guidance and HMRC guidance are all Crown copyright under the **Open Government Licence v3.0**, which allows commercial reuse in a product with attribution. The sources builders actually quote on site, such as British Standards, NHBC Standards, JCT and NEC contracts and the RIBA Plan of Work, are commercial copyright and cannot be ingested without a licence. The paperwork burden is real but unevenly spread: a design-and-build firm carries CDM, building control, CHAS Elite and payment-notice duties, while a sole trader mainly needs quotes, invoices, CIS statements and competent-person certificates. Survey figures put trade admin at roughly 5 to 8 hours a week, mostly done in evenings. Language alone will not be a moat, because any modern model can answer in Romanian; trust, a curated glossary and community distribution would have to be.

## Thirty English tools leave the Romanian layer empty

The market splits into four tiers, and each one is either too big for a small Romanian-run firm or English-only. At the top, **Procore** prices by annual construction volume with no published GBP figures ([Procore UK pricing](https://www.procore.com/en-gb/pricing)) and is adding agents through its Helix layer and an Agent Builder in open beta since October 2025 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251015796723/en/Procore-Advances-the-Future-of-Construction-with-New-AI-Innovations-at-Groundbreak-2025)). Procore's web app supports Polish but **not Romanian** ([Procore support FAQ](https://en-gb.support.procore.com/faq/what-languages-are-available-in-procore?mt-view=f1)). UK construction ERPs such as COINS, Access, Causeway and Eque2 are the only systems that handle CIS natively, and they are estimated at tens of thousands of pounds ([ERP Research UK](https://www.erpresearch.com/en-gb/best-erp-software-for-construction-industry); [Compare the Cloud](https://www.comparethecloud.net/articles/construction-project-software-uk-contractors-cis-rams)). No Romanian builder with five vans buys these.

The real competition for builders_lab sits in the trades-app tier, which costs about **£25 to £45 per user per month**. Tradify charges £34 to £44 per user and puts its AI ("SmartRead" for bills, "SmartWrite" for job text) only on the top plan ([Tradify UK pricing](https://www.tradifyhq.com/uk/pricing)). Powered Now is the only one that advertises HMRC Making Tax Digital and VAT submission with CIS in the app, from £23.33 a month, and sells an AI receptionist at £29 to £39 with a "natural British voice" ([Powered Now pricing](https://www.powerednow.com/pricing)). Fergus has a voice assistant for updating jobs on site ([Fergus UK pricing](https://fergus.com/uk/pricing/)), and Jobber's AI Voice logs field notes "in any language" with translation, but only for team members, not owners ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/en-gb/pricing/)). None of their UK pricing pages lists Romanian. Users rate these apps well (Tradify 4.8 on 625 Trustpilot reviews), and complaints are about gaps, not price: missing reports, Xero sync that "sometimes doubles things up", slow support ([Trustpilot Tradify](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tradifyhq.com)).

The AI-first tier is where the overlap with builders_lab is closest, and it is already busy. **StructureGPT** gives cited answers over Approved Documents A to T for England only, with a free tier of 50 queries a month and paid plans at £19 and £69 ([StructureGPT](https://www.structuregpt.co.uk/)). **BuildingRegs.app** indexes far more: the English and Welsh Approved Documents, Scottish handbooks, CDM 2015 and HSE guidance, BS 7671, NHBC Standards and BREEAM ([BuildingRegs.app](https://www.buildingregs.app/)). Struck covers eight European countries with 81 UK documents ([Struck UK](https://struck.eu/uk/home)), and there is even a free open-source local tool ([GitHub para-building-regs](https://github.com/valtomas/para-building-regs)). At least eight AI RAMS generators exist, from RAMSReady (£29 per document, £49 a month unlimited, £299 for principal contractors) to DocGen, which also drafts a Construction Phase Plan from a site video ([RAMSReady](https://ramsready.co.uk/); [DocGen](https://app.docgen.co.uk/)). AI quoting apps that turn voice, text or photos into a quote are multiplying (QuickPrice, QuotePilot, ToolBoxHQ, ToolsDownAI over WhatsApp), and a merchant like Wickes now publishes a guide to them ([Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/trade/ai-tools-for-tradespeople-summer-26)). Every regulation assistant found is English-only or silent on language, and every one carries a "not professional advice" disclaimer.

The Romanian-built tools confirm the gap from the other side. **eDevize's eVision AI** matches bill-of-quantity lines to Romanian norm recipes under HG 907/2016 ([eDevize](https://edevize.ro/resurse/solutii-dedicate/evision-ai/)), which shows Romanian builders accept AI in estimating but encodes rules useless in the UK. **Abilo**, a construction ERP from Romanian company Obi Systems, sells across the UK from £20 a month with 2,000+ users, but mentions no AI features and plans its Romanian version for Romania, not for UK users ([Revista Biz, Jun 2025](https://www.revistabiz.ro/un-antreprenor-roman-digitalizeaza-constructiile-din-marea-britanie-cu-un-software-in-care-a-investit-peste-16-milioane-de-lire/)). The honest counter-argument is that the gap is shallow: a competitor such as StructureGPT could add Romanian answers in a week, and many Romanian builders probably already paste questions into ChatGPT (unsourced). An ArchAdemia review warns that general assistants have given wrong or outdated Approved Document citations ([ArchAdemia](https://archademia.com/blog/how-architects-are-actually-using-ai-assistants-right-now-and-where-they-still-fall-short/)), and a Korean case study found generic machine translation garbled construction terms enough to create safety risk ([EasyChair](https://easychair.org/publications/paper/6P1Q)). That is the opening: accuracy on terms and clauses, not just the language.

## Open Government Licence makes the statutory core ingestible

The safest RAG corpus is the statutory one, and it is large. legislation.gov.uk states that all content is under **OGL v3.0** ([legislation.gov.uk help](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/help#licence)), and OGL explicitly allows commercial exploitation "in your own product or application", on two conditions: attribution, and no suggestion of official endorsement ([OGL v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/)). The GOV.UK Approved Documents collection, now A to T plus Regulation 7, is under the same licence; Approved Document B was updated on 16 Sept 2026 and T added on 3 Nov 2025 ([GOV.UK Approved Documents](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/approved-documents)). HSE allows reuse of its web and printed Crown material under OGL, excluding the Law Poster suite, the Accident Book and photos or film ([HSE copyright](https://www.hse.gov.uk/help/copyright.htm); [HSE Books copyright](https://books.hse.gov.uk/copyright)). HMRC's CIS and VAT domestic reverse charge guidance, including the VATREVCON manual and VAT Notice 708, sits on GOV.UK under OGL too ([GOV.UK reverse charge guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-domestic-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services); [VATREVCON](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services-manual)).

The paid and private sources matter because builders work to them. NHBC's terms allow downloads "for your internal purposes and/or personal use" and forbid commercial use without a licence ([NHBC terms of use](https://www.nhbc.co.uk/terms-of-use)). BSI owns copyright in every British Standard and licenses use case by case ([BSI IP statement](https://bsigroup.com/about-bsi/legal/intellectual-property-statement); [BSI licensing](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/pages/licensing)). RIBA forbids reproduction without written permission ([RIBA terms](https://riba.org/about-us/terms-of-use)). The legal backdrop gives no cover: the UK's text and data mining exception (s29A) is limited to non-commercial research, and in March 2026 the government confirmed it will not widen it for commercial AI ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2026/3/copyright-and-ai-in-the-uk-implications-for-rights-holders-and-developers); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/blogs/mse-today/2026/tdm-and-training-ai-models)). The Getty v Stability AI ruling (Nov 2025) helps model training more than retrieval, because it turned on whether model weights are infringing copies ([Hamlins](https://hamlins.com/insight/high-court-delivers-landmark-judgment-on-generative-ai-and-copyright/)), while RAG stores and returns real text. The workable pattern is to ingest OGL text in full, store only metadata, clause numbers and links for copyrighted standards, and get legal advice before launch.

Three design consequences follow. Edition dates must be metadata, because transitional rules hang on them: Scotland's April 2026 handbooks apply to warrants submitted on or after 6 April 2026 ([gov.scot](https://www.gov.scot/collections/building-standards/)). The regime is moving: the Building Safety Regulator became a standalone body on 27 Jan 2026 ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://connections.nortonrosefulbright.com/post/102m2l3/building-safety-regulator-key-changes-from-january-2026)), and some Gateway 2 rules changed from 1 Sept 2026 ([NFRC](https://www.nfrc.co.uk/resource/changes-to-the-building-safety-regime.html)). And Romanian source material is too old to be primary: HSE's Romanian CIS59 welfare leaflet dates from 2010 while the English one was updated in Oct 2024 ([HSE CIS59 Romanian](https://www.hse.gov.uk/PUBNS/cis59r.htm)). This supports the ADR 0001 direction: retrieve in English, answer in Romanian, and always show the English clause.

## Paperwork climbs steeply from sole trader to design-and-build

The three user levels share quotes, invoices, client records, insurance and photos, but legal duties stack up as a firm grows. A **sole trader** working as a subcontractor mostly receives CIS deductions, at 20% if registered and 30% if not ([gov.uk: What is CIS](https://www.gov.uk/what-is-the-construction-industry-scheme)), issues reverse-charge VAT invoices if VAT-registered and working for another builder ([gov.uk DRC](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-domestic-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services)), and, in licensed trades, produces competent person scheme certificates for electrics, windows or heating ([gov.uk competent person schemes](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/competent-person-scheme-current-schemes-and-how-schemes-are-authorised)). From 6 April 2026, CIS sole traders with qualifying income over £50,000 also fall under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax ([Clear Books](https://www.clearbooks.co.uk/making-tax-digital-income-tax/builders/)).

A **small builder with a crew of 2 to 10** steps into two duties many do not know they hold. As a contractor paying subbies, it must verify them with HMRC, make deductions, file monthly CIS returns and keep records, with penalties for late returns ([gov.uk CIS contractor duties](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor)); secondary sources put the return deadline at the 19th of each month and the first late penalty at £100 ([GoFile](https://gofile.co.uk/knowledgebase/construction-industry-scheme/penalties/)). And on homeowner jobs, CDM 2015 moves the client's duties to the builder: to the contractor on a single-contractor job, or to the principal contractor on a multi-contractor one, unless a designer agrees in writing to take them ([HSE: domestic clients](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm)). This is likely the most under-met legal duty at this level and a strong use case: a proportionate construction phase plan drafted from a job description, with human sign-off. Off-premises homeowner contracts also carry a 14-day cancellation right ([Business Companion](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/consumer-contracts/consumer-contracts-off-premises-sales)).

A **design-and-build firm** such as Robert's 21st Century Design in Hatfield, which offers design, planning approval, construction and interiors and shows CHAS and CHAS Elite logos ([roberts21stcenturydesign.com](https://roberts21stcenturydesign.com/)), adds the full stack: principal designer and principal contractor roles, building control applications, F10 notification on larger jobs (over 30 working days with more than 20 workers at once, or over 500 person days) ([Designing Buildings](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/F10_notification)), formal variations, handover packs, and annual pre-qualification against SSIP criteria such as H&S policy, risk assessments, method statements and training records ([SSIP Core Criteria](https://ssip.org.uk/ssip-core-criteria/)). On commercial work and subcontracts, the Construction Act's payment notices and pay less notices are statutory; they do not apply to contracts with a residential occupier, an exception the Court of Appeal ruled on for the first time in *RBH Building Contractors v James* [2026] EWCA Civ 511 ([BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/court-of-appeal-guidance-on-pay-less-notices-and-first-ruling-on-the-residential-occupier-exception.html)). That gap is why written quotes, staged payments and signed variations are a homeowner builder's only protection.

The pain is consistent across surveys, though every figure is soft. A 2026 HeyBRB survey of 167 owner-operators (only 13% trades) found 8 hours a week of repetitive admin, 77% of trades respondents doing it in the evenings, over a third spending 20+ minutes per quote, and 16% finding AI output too generic ([Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/uk-tradespeople-losing-ten-working-weeks-a-year-to-admin-they-could-automate-survey-finds/)). A 2021 merchant survey of 700 builders found 62% struggled to keep paperwork in order and only 6% paid someone to do it ([Builders Merchants Journal](https://buildersmerchantsjournal.net/paperwork-is-builders-biggest-stress-merchant-research-finds/)). The FMB/CIOB State of Trade survey of 493 SME firms in early 2026 found only 57% are paid within agreed terms ([FMB](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/construction-smes-face-mounting-cost-pressures-and-insolvencies-despite-continuous-growth-report-warns.html)). Most survey sponsors sell admin software, and none splits results by firm size, so the hours figures are indicative only.

## About 100,000 Romanian builders gather mostly in informal channels

Romanians dominate the foreign construction workforce by a wide margin. The June 2026 ACORD UK and Bournemouth University report cites CITB analysis putting Romanians at **about 5% of UK construction workers and about 19% in London**, with the next foreign nationality under 1% ([undelucram.ro, Aug 2026](https://www.undelucram.ro/en/cumlucram/1-million-romanians-in-the-uk-what-the-workforce-numbers-mean-for-employees-and-recruiters-2435); [Profit.ro, Jul 2026](https://profit.ro/perspective/romanii-au-devenit-motorul-invizibil-in-marea-britanie-parasesc-insa-accelerat-regatul-ritm-de-plecare-mai-mare-decat-al-oricarei-alte-nationalitati-din-ue-22516305); [Bournemouth eprints](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/42193/)). The HBF census of on-site housebuilding in 2023 found Romanians were **6% of all workers and 37% of the non-UK workforce**, concentrated in labouring, plastering and decorating ([HBF Workforce Census 2023](https://www.hbf.co.uk/documents/12623/HBF_Workforce_Census_2023.pdf)). Applied to a workforce of about 2.1 to 2.2 million, 5% suggests roughly **100,000 to 110,000 Romanian construction workers**, a back-of-envelope estimate rather than a published figure. The best self-employment data is old: ONS found 66% of EU2 (Romanian and Bulgarian) construction workers self-employed in 2014 to 2016 ([ESTEEM summary of ONS](https://esteem.sites.sheffield.ac.uk/english/esteem-resources/knowledge-base/report-from-the-ons)), which points to most being CIS sole traders, the level-three user. No count of Romanian-owned construction companies exists, though Companies House shows the pattern ([example](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/14069541/officers)). The population is shrinking: about 37,000 Romanians left and 14,000 arrived in the first half of 2025, the highest departure rate of any EU nationality ([Profit.ro](https://profit.ro/perspective/romanii-au-devenit-motorul-invizibil-in-marea-britanie-parasesc-insa-accelerat-regatul-ritm-de-plecare-mai-mare-decat-al-oricarei-alte-nationalitati-din-ue-22516305)).

English ability is unmeasured for this group. The same report says a third of Romanian adults in England and Wales hold degree-level qualifications and 39% work below their qualification level ([undelucram.ro](https://www.undelucram.ro/en/cumlucram/1-million-romanians-in-the-uk-what-the-workforce-numbers-mean-for-employees-and-recruiters-2435)), while a RICS piece estimates just over one in three site workers may not fully understand instructions or documents ([RICS](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/built-environment-journal/site-safety-risks-language-gap.html)), and HSE has prosecuted a case where training was given only in English ([Enable Law](https://www.enablelaw.com/news-and-insights/protecting-migrant-workers-from-workplace-injury/)). Where the community gathers is mostly informal and hard to measure: Facebook groups and WhatsApp (member counts not retrievable), diaspora media such as Ziarul Românesc and Gazeta Românească ([Romanian Cultural Centre guide](https://www.romanianculturalcentre.org.uk/guide)), the British Romanian Chamber of Commerce ([BRCC](https://brcconline.eu/)) and associations like My Romania Community in Brent ([Brent Council](https://www.brent.gov.uk/en/neighbourhoods-and-communities/community-directory/my-romania-community)). The most direct channel is the **Romanian Builders Network UK**, whose first event in June 2026 at the Romanian Consulate covered qualifications, CIS and VAT, and financial protection, and whose second runs on 21 Nov 2026 in London with 80 places at £35 ([Ziarul Românesc](https://ziarulromanesc.net/prim-plan-uk/constructorii-romani-din-uk-se-intalnesc-din-nou-la-londra-romanian-builders-network-uk-editia-a-ii-a/); [Diaspora RO](https://diasporaro.co.uk/prima-retea-a-constructorilor-romani-din-uk-s-a-lansat-la-londra/)). Its chosen topics are close to a demand signal for paperwork help.

## A starter glossary must bridge two permit systems

The glossary matters most where the two countries' systems do not map one to one. A Romanian *autorizație de construire* is one permit; in the UK it splits into planning permission and Building Regulations approval, and much work needs the second without the first. A *diriginte de șantier* has no exact UK equivalent; compliance checks fall to the building control inspector. A *deviz* is an itemised estimate, but in the UK an "estimate" is not binding while a "quote" is a fixed price. Only a few rows are backed by sources: the CIS rates and the 10-digit UTR ([LITRG](https://www.litrg.org.uk/working/self-employment/construction-industry-scheme-cis); [HMRC CIS301](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/676438734e2d5e9c0bde9c00/CIS301.pdf)), "Rigips" as a brand name for plasterboard ([ProZ](https://wiki.proz.com/kudoz/1902774)), and "builders' merchants" ([ProZ](https://vie.proz.com/kudoz/english-to-romanian/business-commerce-general/7154725-builders-merchants.html)). The rest come from bilingual knowledge, and the Romglish rows are hypotheses; all need a native-speaker tradesperson check. A selection of the 102-row draft follows.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the product question from "can we build Romanian RAG over UK rules" (yes, and the OGL core makes it legal) to "why would a Romanian builder pick this over StructureGPT plus Google Translate". The defensible answer is narrow and specific: answers tuned to the duties small builders miss, above all the CDM domestic-client rule, CIS and reverse-charge invoicing, with every answer tied to an English clause and every term checked against a curated glossary. Licensed-trade and design users are well served in English already; the under-served user is the Romanian CIS subcontractor and the 2-to-10-person crew boss doing admin in the van at night.

Two findings change the risk picture. First, the sources builders cite most (BS 7671, NHBC, JCT) are exactly the ones builders_lab cannot store, so the assistant must be honest about pointing rather than quoting, and this limits how complete it can feel. Second, the community is large but leaving faster than any other EU group, so early traction through the Romanian Builders Network matters more than breadth. Willingness to pay is the biggest unknown: the price anchors (free to about £50 a month) are set by English tools, and no source measures what Romanian builders would pay or how many already use ChatGPT for the same job.
