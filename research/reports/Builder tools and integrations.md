# Connect the builder's inbox, not a platform

Small and medium UK builders, both high-end design-and-build firms like Robert's 21st Century Design and commercial refurbishment contractors like FDT Construction Services, run their day on a light, generic stack: email, a shared drive full of PDFs and Excel trackers, accounts software (Xero or Sage), and WhatsApp for site and client talk. Big construction platforms such as Procore show up mainly at firms with £18M+ turnover, and no public material from either example firm names any software at all. That is good news for builders_lab: most of what these firms already use can be reached from the Claude app today with connectors nobody needs to build (Gmail, Google Drive, Microsoft 365 for search, Dropbox, DocuSign, Xero), while the one thing missing is the glue that knows about jobs: which email belongs to which job, what is still missing before handover, and which payment deadline is next. That glue is our own small MCP server, which is already the core of the 21 Nov prototype. WhatsApp is the biggest gap: it is where variations get agreed, but no connector can read a builder's existing chats, so for the meeting it should be shown as a pasted chat export and named as "coming next". The ranked build list below therefore starts with things we use, not build, and the six proposed demo scenarios map each to a real legal or admin pain with a public source behind it. All facts were checked on 19 Nov 2026; much of the software evidence comes from vendor pages and job adverts, and nothing here tells us what Robert's or FDT actually use. Only interviews can do that.

## Design-and-build firms carry the CDM, warranty and variation paperwork themselves

A firm like Robert's sells one team from concept to keys. Its site says it is "experts in luxury new builds, high end refurbishments & commercial projects" in Hertfordshire since 2008, offers architectural design, planning approval, construction and interiors, and promises to "manage every stage"; it shows **CHAS and CHAS Elite** logos and **Best of Houzz** awards, but no client portal, app or warranty provider ([Robert's 21st Century Design](https://www.roberts21stcenturydesign.com/)). Similar firms describe the same shape. HollandGreen names three build roles: the main contractor's site manager, a contracts administrator or QS who "tracks and approves variations" and signs off payments, and its own project managers coordinating everyone ([HollandGreen](https://www.hollandgreen.co.uk/faq/how-is-the-build-managed/)). Our reading of the journey, not a sourced sequence, is: enquiry, survey, concept (often 3D), planning, building control, detailed design and price, contract, build, variations, snagging, handover with completion documents, aftercare.

The paperwork behind that journey is heavy and lands on the builder. On homeowner jobs, **CDM client duties pass to the contractor** (single contractor) or the principal contractor (several), unless a designer takes them on in writing ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/Construction/cdm/2015/domestic-clients.htm)). CHAS Elite covers **13 risk areas**, and answering the questions is "just the first step": firms must upload evidence that policies are actually followed ([CHAS](https://www.chas.co.uk/contractors/common-assessment-standard/)), and CHAS itself names inconsistent records, outdated documents and evidence spread across sites as the usual failures ([CHAS](https://www.chas.co.uk/blog/why-uk-contractors-struggle-with-common-assessment-standard-and-ssip-compliance/)). New builds and conversions add structural warranty work: LABC Warranty wants plans, dates and building control details up front, stage inspections during the build, and completion documents uploaded before the final inspection ([LABC Warranty](https://www.labcwarranty.co.uk/faqs/warranty-inspections-and-processes)).

The sharpest risk sits in the client chat. In **Jaevee Homes v Fincham (2025)** a series of WhatsApp messages was held to be a binding construction contract ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/whatsapp-construction-contract-held-be-binding-english-law)), and lawyers advise confirming every change in writing and keeping a running change log because agreements happen by text in practice ([Willans](https://www.willans.co.uk/knowledge/building-disputes-advice/)). Survey data says where firms already use AI: in the FMB/CIOB H1 2026 survey of 416 SMEs, **80% of AI users use it for office and admin, 42% for quotes and tenders, 33% for health, safety and quality** ([FMB](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-h1-2026-skills-rising-costs-and-ai-adoption.html)). Houzz's UK survey found **89% of construction pros using AI use it for admin** ([KBB Focus](https://kbbfocus.com/news/6084-new-houzz-report-ai-adoption-grows-across-uk-construction-and-design)).

## Commercial contractors live by CDM and JCT deadlines in occupied buildings

FDT's homepage says it does commercial new build and refurbishment, that "occupied and fully operational job sites are never a problem", and that it runs multi-phase jobs end to end; it names no accreditation or software ([FDT](https://www.fdtconstructionservices.co.uk/)). Its peers sell the same three things: low disruption in live schools, clinics and offices, single-contract design and build, and accreditations such as CHAS, Constructionline Gold and ISO 9001/14001/45001 ([SMG Building Solutions](https://smgbuildingsolutions.co.uk/); [360CE](https://360ce.co.uk/)). None of seven sites checked names its software.

The job runs on two legal frames. CDM sets the safety paperwork: as principal contractor the firm must write a **construction phase plan before work starts**, give every worker a site-specific induction, and check that anyone it appoints has the right skills, which is the legal root of subcontractor vetting ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/cdm/2015/principal-contractors.htm)). Jobs over 30 working days with more than 20 workers at once, or over 500 person days, need an F10 notice ([HSE](https://hse.gov.uk/Construction/cdm/faq/notifications.htm)). JCT sets the money: under JCT SBC/Q, valuations are normally monthly, the payment notice is due **within 5 days of the due date**, a pay less notice must go **at least 5 days before the final date**, and the final date is **14 days after the due date**; without a valid pay less notice the full notified sum is owed ([JCT](https://corporate.jctltd.co.uk/jct-explains-interim-payments/)). Missed notices still drive "smash and grab" adjudications in 2026 ([Fenwick Elliott](https://www.fenwickelliott.com/knowledge-hub/insight/issue-101/)). These timings are for SBC/Q; the Minor Works 2024 timings were not confirmed. At the end, O&M manuals, as-built drawings and the health and safety file are often preconditions to practical completion ([Fenwick Elliott](https://www.fenwickelliott.com/sites/default/files/fenwick_elliott_-_contractual_liabilities_post_completion.pdf)), and the rectification period usually runs 6 to 12 months ([Designing Buildings](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Defect_liability_period)).

Job adverts show the day itself. A November 2026 refurbishment site manager role lists "review and challenge subcontractor RAMS", site inductions, health and safety records, QA checks, progress updates and working "alongside customer liaison teams to minimise disruption to residents" ([The Construction Index](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/construction-jobs/view/-/site-manager-1665/200409)). Money admin is the best-measured pain, though the numbers come from a vendor: Payapps found **67% of UK subcontractors prepare most payment claims in spreadsheets** and 73% had routine disputes over applications ([Payapps](https://www.payapps.com/blogs-ie/download-subcontractor-payment-research-2026-what-contractors-need-to-know-ie/)), and among finance staff **50% use spreadsheets and 46% depend on email** for subcontractor payments ([Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/managing-210-subcontractors-with-pen-and-paper-and-a-spreadsheet/)). CIOB's magazine says WhatsApp is still used for site communication "across the majority of UK projects" ([Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/consumer-messaging-apps-among-project-teams-thumbs-up-or-thumbs-down/)).

## The real stack is email, Excel, accounts and WhatsApp

Direct evidence of small-firm software is thin, and that thinness is itself a finding. A UK SME vendor guide calls **Xero and Sage the "UK SME defaults"** and places Access Coins at mid-to-large contractors with a finance team ([Site Samurai](https://www.sitesamurai.co.uk/alternatives/procore-alternatives-uk-sme-contractors)); it is a competitor of Procore, so treat its framing with care. Procore's own UK case studies start at Kenham, about £18M turnover ([Procore](https://mkt-cdn.procore.com/downloads/casestudies/CaseStudy_Kenham.pdf)). Procore appears as "highly desirable" in a West London high-end refurbishment PM advert ([Construction Job Board](https://www.constructionjobboard.co.uk/job/3531608/project-manager-high-end-residential)) and MS Project or Asta in a Surrey one ([The Construction Index](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/construction-jobs/view/project-managers/project-manager-727/185996)), but small-firm site adverts name qualifications and RAMS, not software. Houzz's UK survey found drafting and rendering software at only **36% of design-and-build firms**, against 76% of architects ([InsightDIY](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/houzz-publishes-2025-uk-state-of-the-industry-report/14904.htm)), which fits a firm like Robert's that partners with outside architects and probably receives drawings by email or shared folder (our inference). Houzz Pro is a plausible tool for a Houzz-award firm, and one UK user says its takeoffs cut a two-to-three-day job to under half a day ([Houzz Pro](https://pro.houzz.co.uk/pro-learn/blog/josh-fortnum-on-how-clever-tech-solutions-help-him)), but that is vendor content and Robert's use of it is not confirmed.

Our best guess for both firm types, labelled as inference: Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace for email, calendar and files; Xero, QuickBooks or Sage for accounts (with a Sage add-on for CIS and valuations at the larger end, see [Sicon](https://www.sicon.co.uk/product/construction/)); Excel for estimates, valuations and change logs; a WhatsApp group per site; PDFs for drawings; and perhaps one field app (Fieldwire, PlanRadar) or, for Houzz-active firms, Houzz Pro.

The connector picture splits cleanly into four groups. The table gives our judgement of how each tool reaches the Claude app.

| WhatsApp | Business Platform only: a new business number, webhook, our MCP; cannot read existing chats or groups | [Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing); [Meta forum](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/1084199161066371/) |
| Buildertrend, Houzz Pro, Tradify, Sage 50, CHAS, Constructionline | No open API (partner-gated, Zapier leads only, Windows COM, or partner data sharing) | [Houzz Pro](https://pro.houzz.co.uk/pro-help/r/what-integrations-does-houzz-pro-support); [Build UK](https://builduk.org/DataSharing) |

Three points shape the ranking. First, the layer that small firms have most, email and files, is already free to us; the only gap is writing to Outlook or OneDrive, which would need our own Microsoft Graph server and only matters if pilot firms are on Microsoft 365. Second, WhatsApp is the highest-value build and the slowest. The API needs a dedicated business number, new portfolios start at 250 messages and need business verification, API groups max out at **8 people** and cannot adopt existing groups ([Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/whatsapp-group-api/)), and since 1 Oct 2026 service replies beyond **1,000 free a month** are charged ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). Unofficial WhatsApp Web bridges carry ban risk and are not product material ([WATI](https://www.wati.io/en/blog/whatsapp-mcp-server/)). Third, construction platforms matter less than their profile suggests: Procore suits larger firms, Buildertrend and Houzz Pro are closed, and Fieldwire's API is a paid add-on ([Fieldwire](https://help.fieldwire.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034986232)).

## Six demo moments map to real deadlines and real legal risk

The prototype (`requirements/prototype.md`) already fixes six live moments on Gmail, Xero and our server. The scenarios below are our proposals for how to frame them for two halves of the room, each tied to a sourced pain. None claims knowledge of what Robert's or FDT use.

| # | Scenario (our proposal) | Firm type | Connectors | Sourced pain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The client said yes on WhatsApp": paste a chat export or forward an email; the assistant writes a variation record (what, price, date, who) and drafts a confirmation email | Robert-type | Gmail, our server (DocuSign optional) | WhatsApp messages can form a binding contract |
| 2 | "When is my next payment notice due?": from contract dates, list valuation, payment notice and pay less dates, and draft the application from the Excel valuation | FDT-type | Our server, Drive or M365, Gmail draft | 5- and 14-day JCT deadlines; smash-and-grab adjudication |
| 3 | "Is this subcontractor ready to start?": check RAMS, insurance and training records in email and the shared folder, flag gaps and expiry dates | FDT-type (and CHAS Elite firms) | Our server, Drive/Dropbox | CDM duty to check competence; CHAS evidence pains |
| 4 | "What's missing before handover?": list missing items against a handover or warranty checklist (electrical certificate, O&M, completion documents) | Both | Our server | O&M and H&S file as PC preconditions; LABC final inspection |
| 5 | "Write today's site diary": turn a day's chat export, photos and notes into a dated diary entry and a short update for the client's facilities team | FDT-type | Our server, Gmail draft | Adverts list site diaries and resident liaison; WhatsApp as the real record |
| 6 | "Make the invoice and chase what's overdue": create a draft invoice in the Xero UK demo company and list overdue invoices with a polite chase email | Both | Xero MCP, Gmail | Money admin is the best-measured pain |

Scenarios 1, 4 and 6 sit on the prototype's existing moments 2, 3 and 6; scenarios 2, 3 and 5 reuse the same server tools (`get_job`, `list_missing_items`, `search_job_documents`) with extra mock records for a commercial job, and can be shown if time allows. The regulations question (moment 4 in the prototype) suits both halves and should stay. Every scenario should end with a draft that a person checks, never a sent email or a compliance verdict, because the paperwork duties stay with the builder by law.

## Conclusion

The research turns the integration question around. The hard part is not reaching a builder's tools: the inbox, folders and accounts of a typical small firm are mostly one click away in the Claude app already. The hard part, and the part nobody sells, is knowing the job: which email is the variation, which date is the pay less deadline, which certificate is still missing. That makes builders_lab's own value a small job-aware server rather than a stack of connectors, and it means a pilot can start with almost no integration cost, so long as the firm uses Gmail or Google Workspace (Microsoft 365 users get search only until we build Graph write tools).

Two facts should shape what is said on 21 Nov. WhatsApp is where the legal risk lives, and it is the one channel we cannot connect without a new business number and Meta approval, so it should be promised honestly as "next", not shown as live. And the room should be asked what they use: no public source tells us whether Romanian-run firms lean on Gmail or Outlook, Xero or Sage, Houzz Pro or nothing, and that answer decides ranks 5 to 9.
