# Asbestos answers sit buried in contractors' inboxes

For a small firm refurbishing council flats in England, the asbestos question is the contractor's own legal problem, not just the council's. **Regulation 5 of the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 says an employer must not start work that may disturb asbestos until it has found out whether asbestos is there, or has presumed it is (and not chrysotile alone)** ([legislation.gov.uk reg 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/632/regulation/5)). The document that answers "where is the asbestos in this flat" is normally a localised refurbishment and demolition (R&D) survey for the room being worked on, laid out to a public HSE template (HSG264, Open Government Licence) with a fixed results table per item. That table is regular enough to extract into structured data, and HSE's own guidance says printed copies usually go to contractors. Councils order work per property, often through a works order priced from a schedule of rates, and each void flat produces a trail of perhaps 10 to 20 documents (our estimate) arriving by landlord portal and by email. The tools on the market split cleanly: landlords and asbestos consultants buy register software (TEAMS, Alpha Tracker, Micad, Asbestos SMART) at unpublished prices, trade contractors can buy job management apps at roughly £45 to £105 per user per month (third-party figures), and Microsoft 365 Copilot can search an inbox in Romanian for about £16 per user per month extra (one UK source). **No product found organises a small contractor's own council emails and attachments by property and answers asbestos questions with the survey page and date cited, in Romanian or in English.** That is the gap. The main risks are that Copilot is "good enough", that council contracts forbid putting council data into a third-party tool, and that a wrong "no asbestos" answer can hurt someone.

## The legal duty lands on the contractor before the first hole is drilled

The law splits asbestos duties in a way that matters for the product. The "duty to manage" in regulation 4 applies to non-domestic premises, and HSE guidance says that includes **the common parts of blocks of flats** (foyers, corridors, lifts, staircases, boiler houses, risers) but not the inside of individual flats ([legislation.gov.uk reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/632/regulation/4); [HSG264, para 10](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)). The regulation itself requires a "written plan" and the passing of location and condition information to anyone liable to disturb it; "register" is HSE guidance wording, not the statute's. Inside the flat, councils still have duties as employers and as CDM clients. Under CDM 2015 the client must give contractors **project-specific** asbestos information, and general warnings are not enough ([HSG264, paras 11–13](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)).

So a contractor refitting a council kitchen holds two duties at once: receive the council's information, and refuse to start (or presume asbestos and work to that standard) if the information does not cover the work. HSE says registers must be checked before work, "including simple and short duration work such as single hole-drilling", and that information should reach contractors at tender or engagement, because giving it "when they arrive on site" leaves no time for controls ([HSG264, paras 146–149](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)). Croydon's housing policy shows how this flows down in practice: contractors must hold awareness training, consult the register, use a licensed subcontractor for work on asbestos materials, "immediately suspend operations" on finding suspect material, return test certificates and waste evidence within 10 working days, and **postpone any job in an unsurveyed pre-2000 property until a survey is done** ([Croydon Asbestos Management Policy, 2025](https://www.croydon.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/asbestos-management-policy-july-2025.pdf)).

Whether the work itself needs a licensed contractor depends on the product, the task and its condition. Sprayed coatings, pipe lagging, loose fill and most work on asbestos insulating board (AIB) are licensable. Removing badly damaged asbestos cement or steaming off large areas of textured coating is notifiable non-licensed work (NNLW), which needs notification before work, medicals at least every three years and a 40-year register of work and workers. Drilling holes in textured coating or painting sound AIB is non-licensed but still needs controls and training ([HSE NNLW page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/licensing/notifiable-non-licensed-work.htm)). Training comes in three levels (awareness, non-licensable, licensable), and awareness training is meant for exactly the trades a refurbishment firm employs: electricians, plumbers, joiners, painters, plasterers ([HSE training](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/training.htm)). The only change found for 2025–2026 is narrow: after a consultation that closed in January 2026, a statutory instrument laid in November 2026 stops removal contractors appointing their own clearance analyst. Survey accreditation stays voluntary and NNLW stays ([HSE consultation outcome](https://consultations.hse.gov.uk/hse/proposals-control-of-asbestos-regs-2012/)). Whether that instrument has been made and when it starts was not confirmed.

Prosecutions show the failure patterns an assistant should help avoid: no refurbishment survey before a flat refit (Newnham College flats, £12,000 plus costs, breaches of regs 5 and 16) ([Housing Compliance](https://www.housingcompliance.co.uk/asbestos-hse-prosecutions-for-failings/)); carrying on after a subcontractor reported asbestos (ASAI Construction, £30,000) ([HSM](https://www.hsmsearch.com/workers-exposed-asbestos-30k-fine)); and a council sending a contractor in without passing on the survey (North Warwickshire, £5,000) ([Irwin Mitchell](https://www.irwinmitchell.com/newsandmedia/2013/november/council-and-decorating-firm-prosecuted-for-asbestos-failings-918009)). Most reported cases are from 2010 to 2021, many details come from secondary news, and no 2025–2026 English council-flat case was found.

## One survey type answers the question, and its table is machine-readable

HSG264 (2nd edition, 2012, still current and free under OGL) defines two survey types ([HSG264 page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/hsg264.htm)). A **management survey** covers normal occupation and foreseeable maintenance, with minor intrusion, and may be partly or wholly presumed. A **refurbishment and demolition survey** is fully intrusive and destructive (lifting floors, breaking into walls and ceilings) and covers only the work area. HSE is explicit: "Refurbishment surveys will be required for all work which disturbs the fabric of the building in areas where the management survey has not been intrusive" ([HSG264, paras 43–53](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)). For housing, landlords group stock into archetypes by build date and design, survey a sample, and treat every unsurveyed home as able to contain any material found in its type; R&D surveys are needed "only ... in the specific area/location where the works will take place, eg cupboard, part of a room, kitchen/bathroom" ([HSG264, paras 60–69](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)). HSE also warns that homes of the same archetype differ because of builders' offcuts, unrecorded removals and tenant changes (para 61).

The practical consequence for the product is that "no asbestos found" is only true for the rooms and intrusion level surveyed. A bathroom R&D survey says nothing about the hallway; a management survey says little about what is behind the kitchen wall. Survey type, date, rooms covered and the "not accessed" list must therefore travel with every answer.

The report layout is standard. HSG264 lists six sections (executive summary, introduction, site and survey information, results, conclusions and actions, bulk analysis) and says the executive summary must name locations with identified or presumed asbestos, specific areas not accessed, high-scoring items and actions ([HSG264, paras 132–136](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)). The per-item results table (HSG264 Table 1) has twelve columns: **location, product type, extent, accessibility, condition, surface treatment, asbestos type, sample number, sampled/presumed/strongly presumed, material assessment score, priority score, action**. Results go room by room, rooms not accessed appear in the table as presumed, and negative results sit in a separate table ([HSG264, paras 137–141 and p.43](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)). The lab appendix carries the lab's UKAS number, the method and results by sample ID (para 143). The register is a simpler "living document" derived from the report and updated when items are removed or change condition (paras 146–147).

The material score is a simple sum of four factors, which makes it easy to check during extraction:

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product type | – | Composites: vinyl tiles, asbestos cement, textured coatings, mastics | AIB, millboard, textiles, gaskets, ropes, paper | Lagging, sprayed coatings, loose fill |
| Damage | Good | Low | Medium | High, debris visible |
| Surface treatment | Composites | Enclosed sprays/lagging, painted AIB, AC sheets | Unsealed AIB, encapsulated lagging | Unsealed lagging/sprays |
| Asbestos type | – | Chrysotile | Amphiboles except crocidolite (e.g. amosite) | Crocidolite |

Totals run 2 to 12: 10 or more is high, 7–9 medium, 5–6 low, 4 or less very low. Presumed items are scored as crocidolite unless there is strong evidence otherwise ([HSG264, paras 125–127 and Appendix 4](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg264.pdf)). Priority scores add occupancy and use and are set by the dutyholder, using HSG227, which was not opened. Materials can be presumed asbestos-free only with strong evidence, and the date cut-offs are post-1985 for AIB and other amphibole products, post-1990 for textured coatings and post-1999 for chrysotile products (para 38). Commercial register software follows this model ([PSI2000 manual](https://www.manula.com/manuals/psi2000/glossary-of-terms/1/en/topic/the-risk-assessment-algorithm)).

No real per-property council survey or register was found online, which is unsurprising for privacy and security reasons, though Sunderland publishes some block-level registers as web pages ([Sunderland, Jopling House](https://www.sunderland.gov.uk/article/27945/Jopling-House-Asbestos-Register)). Registers are also often wrong: one landlord review found over 800 incorrect asbestos risk entries ([Roofing Today](https://roofingtoday.co.uk/landlord-review-unearths-hundreds-of-incorrect-asbestos-records)). An assistant should therefore quote the source document and its date, not state facts of its own. HSE has a Romanian-language page, but none of its Romanian leaflets covers asbestos ([HSE Romanian page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/languages/romanian.htm)), so a Romanian explanation of these checks does not exist from the regulator.

## Each void flat produces a paper trail across portal and inbox

Council property work splits into voids (empty homes made ready to re-let), responsive repairs, planned works such as kitchen and bathroom programmes, and adaptations. Voids and adaptations are where regional and single-trade lots open to small firms. The Procurement for Housing (PfH) DPS for repairs and voids has five lots, from multi-trade to electrical and specialist, and asks only for Gas Safe or NICEIC for the trade lots, leaving other requirements to each landlord ([PfH DPS brochure](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2630870_DPSResponsiveRepairsVoidsWorks-PfH_082225.pdf)). Large bundles go to big contractors, such as Mears' reported 10-year, £450m repairs-and-voids contract at Birmingham ([Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/?p=510467)). Gas is often split out: Runnymede's void standard says "All gas works are to be carried out by RBC's Specialist contractor" ([Runnymede Void Standard](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)).

Small firms get in four ways: by joining a DPS at any time (a landlord can then tender "in as little as 10 days"), by winning a place on a framework with regional and value-band lots, by quoting for a council's own below-threshold work, or by subcontracting to a main contractor. The PfH repairs and voids framework names 75 firms, is worth £725m and runs to July 2027 ([PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/building-control-news/75-contractors-appointed-to-pfhs-reactive-repairs-framework/164349/)); LHC's 2026 H3 framework reserves up to three places for micro or small firms on some lots ([Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/05/01/lhc-launches-1bn-housing-and-demolition-framework/)). Under the Procurement Act 2023, existing DPSs must end by 27 October 2028 at the latest ([Bevan Brittan](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2024/can-dynamic-purchasing-systems-be-used-for-below-threshold-contracts/)), and **30-day payment terms are implied into public contracts (s.68) and carried down to public sub-contracts (s.73)**, though not for disputed invoices ([Ward Hadaway](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/procurement-in-a-nutshell/procurement-in-a-nutshell-supply-chain-payment-terms/)). No source measured how much void work goes to small or Romanian-run firms; that framing is the project's premise.

Work arrives per property as a works order, usually priced from a schedule of rates. The national M3NHF schedule (version 8, 2023) gives each item a six-character code and a priority code, and each rate covers "the entire work content" of the repair, adjusted by a tendered discount; the client's representative decides what is approved ([M3NHF SoR v8 extract](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/Assets/NoticeBuilder_FileDownload.aspx?id=357559)). Peabody's NEC Housing contractor portal lets contractors add notes, vary an order, mark it complete and apply for payment, and each contractor sees only its own orders ([Peabody NEC guide](https://www.peabodygroup.org.uk/media/tqylexo0/nec-contractor-portal-v211.pdf)). CIS deductions of 20% (registered), 30% (unregistered) or 0% (gross status) apply to labour only ([GOV.UK CIS](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor/make-deductions-and-pay-subcontractors)). Whether councils act as CIS "deemed contractors" was not checked.

Runnymede's void standard is the clearest published picture of the document flow. Going in, the contractor gets keys, pre-termination information ("status of electrical, gas and energy efficiency certification, any asbestos information, and predicted remaining lives ... of main components") and the asbestos register, which it must check and brief operatives on. Coming back, it sends meter photos at start and handover, photos of rechargeable items, immediate reports of serious defects, an EICR before remedial instructions and then an electrical installation certificate, an EPC at handover, a gas safety certificate where relevant and a completed handover schedule. The turnaround target is **15 working days**, followed by a joint handover meeting, a "Contractors' Portal" entry and a 12-month defects period ([Runnymede Void Standard](https://www.runnymede.gov.uk/downloads/file/2890/void-standard-policy-2022-25)). Hammersmith & Fulham requires an asbestos survey to be "available or undertaken ... for all properties when they become empty", with the contractor doing enabling works ([LBHF void schedule](https://democracy.lbhf.gov.uk/documents/s123732/Appendix%202%20-%20Void%20schedule.pdf)), and Tendring either sends survey copies or gives access to an online survey database ([Tendring policy 2025](https://tdcdemocracy.tendringdc.gov.uk/documents/s73633/A5%20Appendix%20B%20-%20Asbestos%20Management%20Policy%202025.pdf)). Some items travel outside the portal: Runnymede's meter and rechargeable photos are "passed to the Contract Administrator" by an unstated channel. Portals vary by landlord (NEC Housing, Open Housing, Housing Online's My Home) ([York Open Housing](https://www.york.gov.uk/OpenHousing?docid=home); [My Home for Contractors](https://www.housing-online.com/resources/My_Home_For_Contractors_04.pdf)), and none was found to offer bulk export. Email is the only channel a small contractor fully controls.

Volumes and money give a sense of scale. Social landlord re-let times averaged close to 50 days before falling to about 41, with the vacancy rate at a three-year low of 0.43% in July 2025 ([Inside Housing](https://www.insidehousing.co.uk/news/social-landlords-reduce-void-properties-to-three-year-low-93915)). Average works cost per void, where published, sits around **£2,400 to £2,850**: £2,682 at Ashfield against a £1,950 budget unchanged since 2018 (with "missing contractor invoices" expected to push it higher) ([Ashfield scrutiny, Mar 2025](https://www.ashfield.gov.uk/media/dhhbew21/scrutiny-findings-report-march-2025.docx)), and £2,836 at Cambridge with a turnaround of about 15 days ([Cambridge, June 2026](https://cambridge.gov.uk/media/a5aowzlg/roam-meeting-notes-2026-06.pdf)). The builder interviewed on 7 Nov 2026 described a council budget of about £10,000 for a batch of properties (currency assumed); at these averages that would cover roughly three to four standard voids, but batch budgets were not confirmed in any source ([interview note](../interviews/builder-1.md)). No sourced figure for voids per council per year was found.

## Data protection and Awaab's Law decide what the tool may hold

Councils treat repair contractors as data processors under UK GDPR. A typical schedule says "The relationship between the parties is Controller & Processor", forbids sub-processors without written authorisation and limits use to the stated purpose ([North Northamptonshire schedule](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/Attachment/ecb1afb5-a790-4c3c-8fbf-e49993c5ce88)); data is returned or deleted at contract end "as directed by the Council" ([Barnsley Appendix E](https://barnsleymbc.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s70236/Appendix%20E.pdf)). What contractors receive includes tenant contact details, access information and **vulnerability indicators** ([RBKC DPIA](https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/Committees/documents/s36760/ED5014342R3%20DPIA.pdf)). An AI tool indexing a contractor's council email would almost certainly be a sub-processor needing the council's written consent, UK hosting, per-client separation and a deletion process. That is our reading and needs legal review, and no source says whether councils allow it.

Awaab's Law adds pressure in the other direction. The Hazards in Social Housing Regulations came into force on 27 October 2025, with emergency hazards to be investigated and made safe within 24 hours and significant hazards investigated within 10 working days; landlords must keep records of access attempts and contractor correspondence, and contractors are expected to "supply information for written summaries promptly" ([GOV.UK Awaab's Law timeframes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector)). Phase 2 in 2026 adds fire and electrical hazards and Phase 3 in 2027 covers nearly all remaining HHSRS hazards; the exact Phase 2 start date was not confirmed, and Shelter gives October 2026 ([Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk/professional_resources/news_and_updates/how_awaabs_law_changes_the_rules_on_hazards_in_social_housing)). The reformed Decent Homes Standard will be enforced from 2035 ([GOV.UK response, Jan 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/consultation-on-a-reformed-decent-homes-standard-for-social-and-privately-rented-homes/outcome/consultation-on-a-reformed-decent-homes-standard-for-social-and-privately-rented-homes-government-response)). A contractor who can quickly produce a dated evidence trail per property becomes more useful to a landlord under these rules.

Asbestos register products confirm that the data lives with the landlord. TEAMS gives a contractor a portal view of "the latest reports and ACM details for the specific locations they work in", and its Enterprise version pulls surveys back into the landlord's central register; licence prices are quote-only ([TEAMS portal](https://www.teams-software.co.uk/Portal)). Asbestos SMART lets landlords put register links into work orders ([Asbestos SMART](https://d32j0cd29uv9q5.cloudfront.net/asbestos-smart-take-a-look-at-the-features-and-benefits)), and Access Maintain pushes the asbestos report to engineers' phones and flags sites missing it ([Access Maintain help](https://help-accessmaintain.theaccessgroup.com/en/articles/12889794-managing-asbestos-reports)). An open data standard, OpenAsbestos, is being discussed ([UKATA](https://ukata.org.uk/news/openasbestos-open-standard-asbestos-data-sharing)). A small contractor never buys any of these; at best it gets a login, and more often a PDF in an email.

Trade job management tools are priced for firms that run their whole operation through them. Joblogic's standard tier is about £45 per user per month billed annually, with higher tiers quote-only ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/reviews/134632/joblogic); [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/tools/job-management-software-uk-trades-cost/)), and a November 2026 check found Commusoft, simPRO and BigChange show no UK prices at all ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/tools/job-management-software-uk-trades-cost/)). No page was found showing any of them indexing a contractor's inbox or offering an asbestos field per site. On the AI side, every purpose-built social housing product found is sold to landlords: Made Tech's void prioritisation ([G-Cloud](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/278682754426814)), Unify.ai's void management ([Unify.ai](https://unify.ai/solutions/social-housing/void-management)), and HousingAI, a paid knowledge base on English social housing law launched in April 2026 on UK AWS ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/housingai-launches-ai-knowledge-platform-for-social-housing)). HousingAI proves a domain RAG product can sell in this sector, but it answers "what does the regulation say", not "what does my inbox say about Flat 12".

The real rival is general AI already in the contractor's email. Microsoft 365 Copilot supports Romanian prompts and answers ([Microsoft support](https://support.microsoft.com/topic/94518d61-644b-4118-9492-617eea4801d8)) and costs about **£16.10 per user per month** on top of Business Standard at £9.60 (one third-party UK source) ([Compare the Cloud](https://www.comparethecloud.net/articles/getting-microsoft-365-copilot-working-for-a-20-person-uk-company-without-an-enterprise-agreement)). Gmail's natural-language inbox questions are limited to paid Google AI plans, English and the US ([Gmail Help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16831098)), and NotebookLM caps each notebook at 50 sources with no official Gmail import ([NotebookLM FAQ](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187)). Whether Copilot answers reliably from scanned PDF survey attachments was not tested.

The gap is specific. A contractor needs answers grouped by property even when one email says "Flat 12" and another says "12 X House" or a job number; answers drawn from scanned survey tables, with page and date cited; awareness of survey type and scope, so a bathroom survey is never used to clear a hallway; a refusal to say "no asbestos" when nothing was found; Romanian answers that keep the English source text; and a price below the Copilot benchmark. The first five are where a general tool is weakest. Whether small firms will pay, and whether their councils will let them, is untested.

## Conclusion

The research turns the builder's request into a sharper product rule: the assistant is not answering "is there asbestos", it is answering "**does a valid survey cover this exact room and this kind of work, and what does it list, presume or leave unaccessed?**" That framing comes straight from regulation 5 and HSG264, it matches the failures seen in prosecutions, and it gives a clear eval design: management survey only for a kitchen job, a bathroom survey with work spilling into the hallway, a no-access riser, an unsurveyed pre-2000 flat, a survey older than the last refit. It also means the hardest engineering is not language but property matching and table extraction from scanned PDFs, where HSG264's fixed columns and scoring arithmetic give a built-in check.

The commercial picture is narrower than "no competition". The data sits in landlord systems the contractor does not own, a Romanian-capable general assistant is already sold for £16 a month, and the council contract may forbid the whole idea. The cheapest way to learn which of these kills the product is not more desk research but three things from the interviewed builder: a few anonymised documents, the data protection clause of his council contract, and a count of emails and attachments per property.
